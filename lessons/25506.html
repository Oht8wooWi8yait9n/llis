NASA Lesson #25506: Small, Diverse Operations Teams Produce Efficient and Innovative Operations Solutions

Lesson ID: #25506 (Database ID: 25506)
Date: 2013-07-31
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: Implementation &raquo; Phase E
Topic / Category: Flight Operations, Payloads, Spacecraft, Organizational Planning
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Lesson Learned occurred over an extended period of time during K2 mission development; date shown is around the period of maximum activity as documented in the attached paper

Lesson(s) Learned

During Phase C/D it is prudent to bring in as much expertise as the program can afford to design the spacecraft/mission and also to verify that the design is good. In Phase E programs are typically much more constrained with both time and money. The Kepler operations team had only a few months to develop the K2 mission, and if it had been constrained by the same level of oversight, documentation and formal reviews that normally are placed on a mission development effort, the team would not have succeeded. Giving the small team more authority to develop the mission quickly and use its own judgment about which processes to adhere to and which ones to set aside was absolutely essential to completing the K2 mission development and in-flight testing on time.

Recommendation(s)

  • When facing problems in flight, it should never be assumed that using more people will lead to a more robust outcome.
  • In all mission phases a “tiger team” of uninhibited, focused technical specialists is often used to solve a problem quickly without spending a lot of money. While not appropriate in all cases, K2 has demonstrated that this approach can be used for mission operations in general with great success.
