NASA Lesson #6936: Cost Estimate of the James Webb Space Telescope

Driving Event / Description

A special Program Management Council review was conducted for the JWST replan. The project provided a Joint Confidence Level (JCL) tutorial. The JCL network schedule is prepared without reserves, then resources are loaded without reserves (using a hammock, if necessary), then the risk register is used by the software to do a Monte Carlo simulation of possible outcomes, thereby generating a higher cost and longer schedule than the data put into the software. In other words, the JCL does not know anything about reserves that are put on top of budget and schedule in traditional project management. Rather, the JCL calculates a higher cost/schedule purely on the basis of the risk register.

Lesson(s) Learned

The Stratospheric Observatory for Infrared Astronomy (SOFIA) JCL how-to manual prepared by the SOFIA program and briefed at the PM Challenge, was very helpful to the JWST project, particularly in the details of how to run the JCL software. The JWST program also prepared a JCL how-to manual. The Standing Review Board schedule analyst made the most valuable contributions in the SRB review of the JCL, by providing suggested changes to the schedule logic network and interconnections.

Recommendation(s)

  1. Improve cost estimate documentation and continually update it to reflect earned value management actual costs and record any reasons for variances.
  2. Before the SRB review, the SRB chair should talk to other SRB chairs experienced in reviewing JCLs, so as to get lessons learned.
  3. Prior to an SRB review of a JCL, the membership of the SRB should be reviewed to ensure there is sufficient experience to review the JCL.
  4. A general training session HQ should be done for HQ senior management, covering how a JCL is prepared, the statistical nature of the Monte Carlo results, how to interpret the results, and the benefits and limitations of the model and methodology.
