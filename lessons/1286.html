NASA Lesson #1286: Co-management of Projects by Non-civil Servants

Lesson ID: #1286 (Database ID: 1286)
Date: 2002-01-01
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Due to circumstances involving human resources and hiring issues, that prevented the Project from assigning a Civil Servant Deputy, a Contractor was placed in the position of Associate Project Manager. However, contractors cannot be responsible for certain management duties such as budget issues or direction of Civil Service employees. Therefore, the Project had to ensure that the proper protocol and authority hierarchy was followed under this unique Civil Servant/Contractor management team.

Lesson(s) Learned

The Project's use of Civil Servant and Contractor personnel allowed the Project to successfully address all its' management responsibilities and roles. The factors that contributed to the success of this unique management team were; an explicit separation of government/contractor (budgetary and personnel) only functions, close and supportive relationship between Civil Servant Project Manager and Contractor Associate Project Manager; and appropriate delegation of authority in the Project manager's absence.

Recommendation(s)

Projects can and should consider filling management positions with contractors when such choices best serve the project's interests and enhance success. However, under this type of management structure, the boundaries of authority and responsibilities must be explicitly defined, communicated, and implemented to avoid unlawful delegation of government authority.
