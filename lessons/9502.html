NASA Lesson #9502: Systems Engineering Management Plan (SEMP) Development for the Space Launch System (SLS) Program

Driving Event / Description

MSFC EV71 is the Technical Management Branch in the Systems Engineering and Integration Division of the Spacecraft and Vehicle Systems Department in the MSFC Engineering Directorate.  EV71 started writing the SLS Program’s SEMP under the direction of the SLS Lead Systems Engineer (LSE) in October 2010.  As step number one, EV71 and the SLS LSE wrote down and agreed upon the following guiding principles for development of the SLS SEMP:

  • Keep SEMP as short and concise as possible by focusing tightly on MPR 7123.1.
  • Help readers see the big picture.
  • Make the SEMP modular and flexible.  See Recommendation #2d on page 4.
  • Avoid duplication.

The Developer of the SLS Program’s SEMP then wrote and revised six drafts before the SLS LSE and SLS Program Control Board (PCB) agreed to internally baseline the draft SEMP (prior to Center approval) on December 3, 2011.  The SLS PCB was challenged with a lack of agreements on the philosophical approach for systems engineering management; the SLS Program Integration Office non-concurred and presented the following concerns:

  • “SEMP may not provide enough specific details for how the SLS Program will integrate the technical effort…”
  • “SEMP may not meet Center and Agency requirements.”
  • “SEMP may not pass external review…”
  • “Missing content may require development of additional documents…”
  • “Pointer documents (MPRs, MPDs, MWIs, etc.) may need to be provided and could be put under scrutiny by external reviewers.”

Some stakeholders wanted the SEMP to be “perfect” but program plans and SEMP are never perfect.  The SLS PCB listened to the non-concurrence and then decided to move forward with approval of the draft SEMP.  The PCB acknowledged that they might have to update the SEMP in the future to reflect new developments as the Program matures.

Another issue that persisted throughout the writing of the SEMP was that decisions were not always made and communicated in open forums.  The closed forums-approach precluded the SEMP writer from capturing some decisions first hand and in context.  The context of the decision can be as important as the decision itself.

The first Engineering Management Council (EMC) was held on January 2012, and the draft SEMP was approved.  Afterward, the SLS Program Manager decided to postpone the SEMP’s presentation to the MSFC Program/Project Management Advisory Committee (PPMAC) and the MSFC Center Management Council (CMC) until after the SLS Program’s combined System Requirements Review (SRR)/System Design Review (SDR).  The SLS Program Manager felt that it would be advantageous to allow the draft SEMP be reviewed at SRR/SDR first so that SRR/SDR Review Item Discrepancies (RIDs) and SRR/SDR Standing Review Board (SRB) Requests for Action (RFAs) could be incorporated in the SEMP before asking the Center Director to approve it.  The SLS Program’s Block 1 SRR/SDR was conducted in February to March 2012.

After SRR/SDR when the draft SEMP was updated, the October 2012 review of the SEMP by the PCB went much smoother. The Program approved the updated draft SEMP on 4 October 2012.

The SLS SEMP was finally approved by the MSFC Center Director in May of 2013 in the CMC, just weeks prior to the kickoff of the SLS Program’s Preliminary Design Review (PDR). The sequence of events from first draft to final approval highlighted some successful areas and other areas for improvement.

Final approval timeline of events:

4 October 2012  SLS Program Control Board approved the Post-SRR/SDR updates to the SEMP.

23 April 2013     Engineering Management Council (EMC)

2 May 2013        Program Project Management Advisory Committee (PPMAC); PPMAC approved

13 May 2013      Modified EMC SEMP presentation presented to Marshall Space Flight Center Management Council (CMC); CMC approved/Center Director approved

30 May 2013      SLS Program Control Board (PCB); PCB accepted the Center’s modifications (they did not elect to reclama any of the changes).

18-19 June 2013             SLS Preliminary Design Review Kick Off

 

At the EMC meeting on 23 April 2013, the Engineering Management Council, chaired by the Deputy Manager of Engineering agreed to the SLS Program’s request that the Center Director delegate authority to the Program Manager (PM) to approve future revisions to the SEMP with the Chief Engineer’s (CE) concurrence.  The SLS Program also requested that the SLS Program would request for the CMC's approval if a future SEMP revision proposes any further tailoring of Center-level requirements. The SLS Program recognizes that the SEMP must comply with MPR 7123.1. If the SEMP changes do not affect the tailoring of the SEMP, the changes can be approved by the Program Manager. 

Note: Tailoring refers to the compliance of the plan to meeting all the requirements from MPR 7123.1 which are traceable to the requirements of NPR 7123.1.  Customization of a SEMP, which is not tailoring, refers to the choices of methods, tools, and processes for complying with the MPR requirements.  Tailoring refers to adjusting the requirements to meet specific program or project needs, and necessarily involves seeking relief from some aspect of the requirement (i.e. a deviation or waiver).   Customization refers to how a requirement (which may or may not have been tailored) is met.

The SEMP arrived at the PPMAC on 2 May 2013.   The PPMAC only addressed comments to the SEMP, and approved the SEMP on 2 May 2013.  The CMC then approved the SEMP on 13 May 2013. 

End Result: Success

 

Additional Information & Comments:

The SLS Program released revision A of their SEMP on December 5, 2013.  The SEMP now has zero To Be Determined (TBD) items.  The SEMP’s Forward Work Plan (Appendix C) is to make updates prior to each lifecycle review to reflect Program maturity, if necessary; and to close any Review Item Discrepancies or Standing Review Board Requests for Action after those lifecycle reviews, if the SEMP receives any.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Good practice – The Developer/Book Manager and the SLS LSE wrote down and agreed upon a set of guiding principles for development of the SEMP before they began writing the SEMP.  The guiding principles were very beneficial and a positive for the Program.  See Recommendation #2
  2. Observation – SEMPs will never be perfect, and that’s okay.
  3. Good practice – Given that a plan is simply a collection of decisions, the Developer/Book Manager of the SEMP actively collaborated with the SLS Lead Systems Engineer and others to help them make those decisions.  The Developer did not passively sit and wait for other people to come to him.
  4. Observation - Document change processes and review times were not fully developed through most of the SEMP development.
  5. Improvement - Include the SEMP author in all meetings where decisions relevant to SEMP content are made, and include context for the decision made.

Recommendation(s)


 
  1. From the beginning, write down the guiding principles to be used for the SEMP’s development and seek agreement from key decision makers.
  2. For example, here are the guiding principles that we adopted for the development of the SLS Program’s SEMP:
  1. Keep the SEMP as short and concise as possible by focusing tightly on the MPR 7123.1 requirements (center requirements for systems engineering).
  2. Help readers see the big picture
  1. Focus on the forest, not the trees
  2. Focus on how the Chief Engineer and the Lead System Engineer will manage their day-to-day system engineering and integration efforts
  1. Make the SEMP modular and flexible
  1. Point to other documents and plans
  2. By analogy, follow a software-like model
  1. Small, central core
  2. Call subroutines (in our case, existing MSFC processes and other SLS plans) to perform detailed instructions
  3. Subroutines (the other plans) can be upgraded without affecting the core
  1. Avoid Duplication
  1. Do not quote verbatim what is written in higher level documents
  2. Do not duplicate details in lower level documents; reference and point to them
  1. SEMP Writers:
  1. SEMP writers need to have good people skills and emotional intelligence to persuade and influence others to make the decisions that a SEMP is required to document. 
  2. It would be very beneficial for a SEMP developer to be a Chief Engineer or Lead systems Engineer at one point in their career so that they have the a) experience, b) know the ground rules, c) know the standpoint or view of the CE, and d) are able to write short, concise requirements. The SLS Program’s SEMP Developer served in both of those roles at one point in his career.
  1. Training
  1. SEMP writers should take the Center-recommended class in “Developing and Implementing a SEMP” BEFORE they are tasked with writing a SEMP
  2. Look for examples to emulate good practices; don’t emulate poor practices
  1. The Review and Approval Process
  1. Plan adequate time for review of the documents prior to major reviews. 
  2. Plan for delays due to changes, or other extenuating circumstances
  3. The SEMP approval presentation preparer should include charts in back up that described decisions or recommendations made at earlier approval meetings.
  4. Identify options for release (e.g. the PCB may have been an effective alternative)
