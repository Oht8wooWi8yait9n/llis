NASA Lesson #17501: Thorough Assessment of Facility Condition During Project Planning and Budgeting

Lesson ID: #17501 (Database ID: 17501)
Date: 2016-09-27
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Not Specified
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Configuration Management, Logistics, Program and Project Management, Logistics and maintenance, Configuration control and data management, Configuration Change Control
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

During the design and development phase of the Ground Systems Development and Operations (GSDO) Program there were a number of instances where unexpected costs and schedule delays resulted from discovering the following types of unexpected issues: Lead-based paint, the presence of asbestos, discovery of structural inadequacy of existing systems, and the discovery of hidden corrosion. Specific driving events:

1.    During refurbishment of Kennedy Space Center Launch Pad 39B, unexpected corrosion was found in various locations including inside the water tank for the Pad water deluge system. This water tank is very large and the corrosion was a major impact.

2.    During modifications to the work platforms in the Rotation Processing and Surge Facility (RPSF)

  • Discovery of Lead-based paint resulted in schedule delays and cost increase
  • Discovery of inadequacies in existing platforms during implementation of modifications resulted in cost and schedule impacts due to repair and reinforcement
  • Unplanned disassembly of decking was required in order to paint the platforms

3.    During modification of the Crawler Transporter, asbestos was found in the control room ceiling.

4.    During the Vehicle Assembly Building refurbishment, the condition of elevator motors and Environmental Control System supply piping were not understood during budget estimation

Lesson(s) Learned

Programs and Projects can be substantially impacted by unexpected additional work resulting from unforeseen issues with reuse of heritage facilities and systems. Thorough assessments of heritage systems need to be performed early, updated as required during the project life cycle and used as a Basis of Estimate for budget and schedule. The assessments should include thorough inspections and analyses. 

Recommendation(s)

  1. Program/Project Managers relying on the use of heritage hardware, facilities and infrastructure should perform a thorough assessment of the capabilities and condition of these assets early in the Program/Project.
  2.  Assessments should consider the following:

a)    The extent of rework required to comply with changes in codes and regulations (OSHA, National Electrical Code, Pressure Vessel System Code, etc.)

b)    Existing issues that will require mitigation; e.g., corrosion, environmental remediation, the presence of asbestos or lead based paint in facilities

c)    Supportability and maintainability analyses, considering issues such as parts obsolescence. 

d)    The assessment should document all assumptions related to facility conditions, required maintenance, rework and supportability of facility systems (supportability of older systems is sensitive to project delays as obtaining repair parts becomes more difficult over time)

e)    For systems including software, consideration should be given to the computing platform, operating system and maintaining of any COTS or custom applications

f)     Address time sensitivity of this analysis (if the project incurs significant delays how the assessment will be changed)

3.    The condition assessment should include physical inspections of visible as well as hard-to-access areas (e.g. hidden            corrosion inside a tank or metal structure)

4. The assessment should be updated in the event that the need date for the heritage systems slips as this may invalidate          the previous analysis
