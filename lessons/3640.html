NASA Lesson #3640: Ground Lightning Monitoring System (GLMS)

Lesson ID: #3640 (Database ID: 3640)
Date: 2010-08-27
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Implementation
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Aircraft, Ground Equipment, Ground Operations, Test and Verification, Entry Systems, Ground processing and manifesting, Ground support systems, Launch support systems, Crew operations and support concepts, Early requirements and standards definition, Review systems and boards, Mission definition and planning, Systems analysis - cost analysis
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Existing lightning instrumentation systems, the Lightning Induced Voltage Instrumentation System (LIVIS) and the Catenary Wire Lightning Instrumentation System (CWLIS), were originally assessed as noncritical (nonconfigured) systems but were later reassessed as critical. Several deficiencies were noted during the reassessment: lack of redundancy and design to satisfy a critical system requirement, inability to detect system failures, no remote health-monitoring capabilities, and lack of certification and configuration control for the software application. Because LIVIS and CWLIS provided only limited insight into vehicle systems, other labor-intensive and time-consuming processes for collecting and evaluating data were necessary to ascertain vehicle health. GLMS was developed to improve insight into the health of vehicle systems and to do so faster and with fewer resources, but it required further validation to meet KSC requirements.

Lesson(s) Learned

GLMS lacked adequate design requirements. In some cases, requirements were written to the operation specifications of hardware that had already been purchased. Hardware was deployed before the system operation was fully understood. System functions were still being tested and undergoing design changes during attempts to certify the system for critical use. GMLS failed to satisfy the requirements of a critical system and did not provide data that could be completely understood or accepted without question.

Recommendation(s)

Entertain a COTS solution if at all possible. If a custom solution is chosen for implementation, ensure that the research and development are complete and independent of the certification process.
