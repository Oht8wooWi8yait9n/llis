NASA Lesson #1445: Accident Investigations/Database/Collaborative Tool PBMA (Process Based Mission Assurance)

Lesson ID: #1445 (Database ID: 1445)
Date: 2003-08-31
Organization: Johnson Space Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

During the Columbia accident investigation, Information Technology (IT) and Configuration Management (CM) implementers assumed that the PBMA would meet all the CM/electronic library tool needs of the investigation and expectations versus delivery caused frustrations. IT issues became backlogged and personnel were frustrated when their issues with the key CM software and Board management tool were not addressed in a timely fashion.

Lesson(s) Learned

Do not assume that a single CM/electronic library tool will meet needs of all investigation participants.

Recommendation(s)

  • Preplan the capabilities needed and the current tools available to quickly and easily begin Board and Task Force operations. Widespread long-term buy-in and use by the investigation and support teams of a single on-line CM/electronic library capability for action tracking and document search & retrieval may not be attainable.
  • Test the interfacing capabilities of the tools annually.
  • Avoid unknown tools. Complex tools will create backlogs of issues and training nightmares.
  • Do not under-estimate the constant care and feeding required to address IT availability, access control, and security issues. IT availability, access control, and security issues were numerous during startup and although less frequent later on, still were persistent. When these issues arose, everyone's issue becomes priority 1, which is understandable but difficult to manage. On-site, adequate co-located support will catch some of these before they became larger issues.
