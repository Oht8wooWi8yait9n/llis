NASA Lesson #2576: Inadvertent Helicopter Motion During Engine Dry Sequence For Routine Wash

Lesson ID: #2576 (Database ID: 2576)
Date: 2010-03-12
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Not Applicable &raquo; Pre-Phase A
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Mission Operations and Ground Support Systems, Safety and Mission Assurance
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The mechanic failed to adjust anti-torque pedals as the throttle was advanced above Flight Idle. The mishap investigation revealed there was no requirement to advance the throttle above Flight Idle for the engine wash/dry procedure.

Lesson(s) Learned

The primary lesson learned from the helicopter mishap was the formalization of seemingly mundane tasks such as washing a helicopter and to require qualified helicopter pilots to perform engine runs.

Recommendation(s)

Require qualified helicopter pilots to perform engine runs and develop formal training sessions to ensure safety.

Evidence / Additional Context

The mishap briefing, corrective actions and lessons learned were briefed to the KSC Center Director and senior staff and at NASA’s Annual Aviation Safety Officer meeting and NASA’s Inter-Center Aircraft Operations Panel at NASA Headquarters which is attended by NASA Senior Aviation staff members and Chiefs of Flight Operations from the NASA centers.

The new requirement for qualified helicopter pilots to perform engine runs was incorporated into the Kennedy Space Center Aircraft Maintenance Manual.
