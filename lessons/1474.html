NASA Lesson #1474: Accident Investigations/Public Inputs to the Investigation/Utilization of a Public Website & Toll-Free Number

Lesson ID: #1474 (Database ID: 1474)
Date: 2003-08-31
Organization: Johnson Space Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The Columbia Accident Investigation Board (CAIB) launched a public website (http://caib.us/) in late February 2003 to inform the public about the activities of the CAIB. A Website link allowed the public to submit information directly to the CAIB through a pop-up form with document attachment capability. An 800 number was also offered as an alternative means to submit information.

In concert with the launching of the Website, a public submission database was developed to receive and review public inputs. This database eventually was expanded to receive submissions from the Website, toll-free number, faxed messages, digital emails, and mail received by the CAIB.

Over a period of two months, the functionality of the database increased incrementally. The first functions served to enable categorization of the submissions by priority and an ability to print records. These were then hand-carried directly to relevant group for action. Excel spreadsheets were used to review and track the database. Later, review and submission of actions to groups was automated. Finally added was the ability of groups to manage their actions and close them in a collaborative environment.

The Process:

Submission Entry:
Public submissions come from several sources and are placed in the database through automatic Website entry or through a CAIB data entry support person. The Public website allows anyone to submit a message, as much information on themselves as they wish, and to attach any relevant electronic document. Those submitting information through the Website receive a message of thanks and a note that they will be contacted if their information needs additional follow-up.

Toll-free phone messages are transcribed and then uploaded into the database with its audio file. Mail submissions are logged at the CAIB in Houston, and then scanned into the database in a PDF file form. Finally, any other hard copy information including emails is copied into the database and given its own unique record.

Data Record Review and Action Assignment:
Throughout each day, data records are read in entirety to determine relevance for action to one or more CAIB groups. A knowledgeable reviewer with senior management and aerospace technical expertise assigns a data record for action if, in his opinion, one or more of the CAIB working groups should see it.

Every data record that has no action assigned is given a second review to make sure that there have been no accidental omissions of a useful submission. If the second review agrees no action is needed, the record is considered closed.

Open records with actions assigned are immediately available to the working groups for disposition. A group manager can assign data records for action to other members of their group. When complete, the comments and action taken can be appended to the data records. Multiple group members can add comments to an action, which also becomes part of the data record.

Statistics:
Statistics about each group and the collective database are automatically and continuously updated as data records are received, reviewed, assigned for action, or closed. These statistics are reviewed each day to help manage the CAIB internal processes for reviewing and closing actions.

Public Submission Information

Statistics
The Public Input Database contains over 3000 records, which were submitted in a little over 75 days. Out of this number over 750 records or 25% were deemed to be important enough for follow-on action by one or more CAIB groups. Surprisingly, given the highly public nature of the CAIB, an overwhelmingly high percentage of the submissions were well-intentioned comments to help the CAIB in its work.

The number of database records increased consistently on a daily basis. After press releases or public hearings, there was usually a large increase in the number of public submissions. Of particular note, many experts would provide their submissions after noting a particular public discussion. At other times, the public information would joggle the memory of former Shuttle or NASA workers about their direct experience and result in a useful submission.

The percentage of submissions, which required action, also changed as the Board progressed. Early in the investigation, there were many avenues that were still in the exploration phase, as well as requests for debris sightings. Consequently, an early wave of theories, opinions, and potentially useful information was categorized as an action. As the Board investigation progressed and possibilities were narrowed, the number of submissions deemed actionable decreased and remained relatively flat. However, there continues to be very useful submissions provided to the CAIB. As the investigation avenues narrow and public information is released, the number of action items should decrease over the rest of May and into June.

Included with these submissions were several documents containing information that the submitters wanted the CAIB Board to investigate or consider.

Nature of Submissions
All database records were reviewed and categorized as being technical in nature, a comment, suggestion, request for information, offer of support, debris sighting, first-hand witness account, or other. Among these categories, there were several topics or themes that evolved during the course of the CAIB investigation. The nature of the submissions also changed as new information was presented in press briefings or public hearings. In fact, the public hearings, which were broadcast and viewable live or delayed through the Website, generated a number of post-hearing submissions with relevant follow-up information and leads.

Themes and topics of submissions included:

Photo and video of the last minutes of Columbia's flight
Debris sightings with pictures attached

Space physics issues to be explored for contributing factors in accident
Identification of experts and facilities in specialty fields relevant to the investigation
History of Columbia construction and operation
Safety and Quality Assurance issues
NASA Management and Culture issues
Technical issues and insights with engineering, materials, or design of Shuttle system
Theories and opinions on cause of accident
Suggestions for improving the Shuttle and return to flight

Hoaxes and Grievances

As with any public access, there were also several categories of submissions that were clearly not helpful to the CAIB, yet were valuable to know. For example, several weeks into the investigation, a widely dispersed Internet hoax claiming Israeli spy pictures were available of Columbia's destruction was promulgated throughout the world. The CAIB public submissions started as a trickle of questions asking if these pictures were real. Several people submitted these pictures through the Website. The CAIB was able to quickly determine they were photos from the movie “Armageddon.” When the volume of inquiries to the CAIB website began to escalate, CAIB Public Affairs agreed to place a statement on the Website stating that the only authorized photos from the investigation would be released by the CAIB. This statement quickly resulted in a marked decrease in these hoax picture inquiries.

Some submitters also felt compelled to submit the same information to the CAIB each day hoping for a direct response. Eventually, the provision for an email reply sharply decreased the number of duplicate and follow up submissions.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Future public investigations should not hesitate to seek information from the public. There will be many valuable submissions from not only the average person, but also from many former experts who wish to contact the Board.

  3. A complete submission response plan is required to utilize appropriately the valuable information that might come in. This would include
a. The ability to evaluate, prioritize, and categorize multiple submissions.
b. Single place for various formats-letters, web, fax, email to be stored in a common record format.
c. Single individual with full knowledge of the areas of investigation, the subject matter, and history of submissions to make sound judgments on disposition of submissions.
d. A second independent review of all submissions for correct disposition.
e. A collaborative environment for subject matter experts or investigators to transfer, evaluate and act upon submissions.
  1. The use of public submissions and follow-up by investigators is dependent on the commitment of the investigation leaders to review submissions in a timely manner and treat them as an important source of information. This must be conveyed to the investigating team.
  2. Policy decisions should be made early in an investigation to determine to what extent all public submissions, in whatever form, should be responded to; either personally, or by automatic reply.
  3. The Public Database should be web-based and secure.
  4. It is important to make progress in reviewing submissions daily in order not to fall behind in review.
a. If there are functions to be tailored, created, or developed, find a mechanism to review and evaluate actions in parallel with development.
b. Excel is valuable decision tools to keep the momentum in reviewing submissions- make sure the database elements can be downloaded to Excel.
  1. It would be helpful if the reviewer of actions has access to the latest thinking and activities of each group. This would have precluded a number of actions for the groups to work on due to the activity already being under consideration.
  2. Responses to the actions were not consistent within each group. It is important to set an early framework for the expectations and degree of response to each action. For example- some closed actions without comments, others detailed at least a reason why the action was closed.

Recommendation(s)

The use of a website and toll free number for public inputs should be considered for all future large accident investigations. The process used by the CAIB should be used as a starting point to document this process. Consideration should be given to documenting this in the Contingency Action Plan (CAP).
