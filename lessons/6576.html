NASA Lesson #6576: Funding opportunities that require quick response times

Lesson ID: #6576 (Database ID: 6576)
Date: 2012-08-01
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: Evaluation
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Topic / Category: Acquisition / procurement strategy and planning, Organizational Planning, Program planning / development and management
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The 80-AS High Temperature Helium (HTH) Project was aimed at developing and operating a unique wind tunnel facility whereby high temperature helium would be the working fluid. The Project's life cycle was relatively short and had to rush its cost and schedule estimates to meet the Constellation Program's decision deadline. Furthermore, this was a project that was formulated in reverse of traditional development efforts in that the requirements were made to fit within the available funding, as opposed to the requirements establishing the required funding. In effect, the project was asked if they could develop the system and perform the tests given a specific dollar amount. Rather than risk losing the funding opportunity, the project quickly responded with a baseline Project plan that was based on what was believed to be a 30% design. However, the reality was a design that was less than 30% due to the rushed conceptualization and the Construction of Facilities (COF) inexperience of the initial Project manager, which was compounded by the complexity of the required system. This was not a typical COF project in that significant modifications to an existing wind tunnel were necessary in addition to the development of the unique helium system. Thus, the main cost drivers the Project experienced were centered on the unique aspects of the wind tunnel facility and included the helium delivery system, the helium supply, and the LAS model; all of which were under estimated when the project was approved. While all this resulted in cost and schedule growth, the Project met all its technical requirements and provided critical aerothermodynamic data for the Orion Launch Abort System (LAS). The primary approaches used to recover from the initial mismatch between the available budget and the required resources to achieve success were infusing the project with experienced personal, working with the customer to refine the requirements, and descoping the Project commensurately with the refined requirements.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Short term funding opportunities may dictate, or tempt, a less than thorough or mature cost and schedule estimate.
    1. Proposals will have to balance the possibility of being turned down for being too costly with being approved with insufficient resources.
    2. There is a certain gamesmanship and constant advocacy required to convince the funding Program to fund a project at a level appropriate to meet all objectives.
  2. Rushing an estimate on a design that is already under conceptualized (less than 30% design) will result in significant cost and schedule growth.
  3. Unique systems and processes will be the most difficult to estimate and will require the largest budget and schedule contingencies.
  4. Pulling an initially under estimated project back into an acceptable scope/cost/schedule box is dependent on the project's ability to negotiate a descoped set of requirements with the customer.

Recommendation(s)

  1. When developing project cost and schedule estimates, ensure the maturity of the concept/design is understood and that the funding organization understands the level of uncertainty and potential for cost/schedule growth that this represents.
    1. Include the worst, best, and most likely estimates, and the risks of exceeding each estimate.
    2. Ensure the uncertainty of any unique item is reflected in each estimate. This uncertainty should be propagated throughout the project lifecycle and include: design, acquisition, fabrication, testing, storage, handling, and operations, as unique items may have special requirements in each of these areas.
    3. Ensure that the project is advocated for by experienced people who know the art of negotiation, specifically with the funding source in question
  2. Cost and schedule estimates based on immature designs should be padded, or over-budgeted, to account for inevitable growth, and be accompanied with descope options.
  3. Budget and schedule contingencies should be the largest for unique items
  4. Projects should have descope options fitting the level of scope/budget/schedule uncertainty represented by the baseline plan and a strategy for when and how to exercise them such that the customer will remain supportive.
