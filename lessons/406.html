Lesson ID: #0406 (Database ID: 406)
Date: 1996-06-18
Organization: Stennis Space Center
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

On Feb. 8, 1996 at 10:04 AM on the B-1 Test Stand, a vent line overpressurization and subsequent rupture occurred. No personnel injuries resulted. Failure damage included the ruptured piping and deformed pipe supports.

Lesson(s) Learned

It is essential for unspent fuel being exhausted through vent lines to be kept as free of oxygen as possible. In the case of this mishap, it was found that air was being intrained in the vent system via an undetected rupture disc vaccuum break. This air, which liquified after "puddling" in vent line low points, in combination with the routinely vented gaseous hydrogen, and a flame front emanating from a downline flare stack created an overpressurization event which thereby compromised the piping.

Recommendation(s)

Several corrective actions were taken:

  1. Suspect rupture disk removed from system with no safety impact
  2. O2 meter installed in vent line piping
  3. New vent line per SSC/NASA standards
  4. Enhancement of SSME drying operations to allow for blanket fuel system purge
  5. Inspection of all rupture disks in use on the B-1 Test Stand

Evidence / Additional Context

The O2 sensor, located optimally for detecting either diffused or stratified oxygen, has experienced no indication of oxygen concentration spikes the in the system. At the trigger point, equal oxygen to 0.5% oxygen concentration, the meter both produces an audible alarm and an automatic gasseous nitrogen purge is initiated.
