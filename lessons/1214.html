NASA Lesson #1214: Capture of Observations from Completed Missions for Post-Flight Follow-up and Lessons Learned

Lesson ID: #1214 (Database ID: 1214)
Date: 1999-02-01
Organization: Johnson Space Center
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Mission teams tend to be re-assigned to other priorities before a complete post-flight or post-mission follow-up are completed, causing delays and loss of important observations and assessments from each mission. In addition, there is a significant amount of work to completing post-mission reviews that need to be done by the outgoing LPM team.

Root cause: Pressure to re-assign personnel to other priorities immediately at the end of a mission; results in post flight follow-up and lessons learned documentation not being completed in a timely fashion.

Lesson(s) Learned

Time must be provided to document lessons learned data from the team that completed a mission while it is fresh in order to provide the most accurate reflection of information to succeeding missions.

Recommendation(s)

Retain LPM team intact until ~6 weeks after mission to complete post flight reviews, action items and lessons learned documentation. Individuals and teams should not wait until the mission or project is done to begin inputting lessons learned. This should be an ongoing part of their jobs so that the post mission close out is not relied upon so heavily to capture all the lessons, many of which will be forgotten along the way if not documented as they occur.
