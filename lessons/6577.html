NASA Lesson #6577: The Preservation and Performance of Lean Project Teams

Date: 2010-01-01
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: Not Applicable
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Topic / Category: Communications between different offices and contractor personnel, Organizational Planning, Program planning / development and management
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The 80-AS High Temperature Helium (HTH) Project was aimed at developing and operating a unique wind tunnel facility whereby high temperature helium would be the working fluid. The Project’s life cycle was relatively short and had to be implemented in a very lean manner necessitating that it be implemented without either a project or systems engineer. This put the Project Manager (PM) in the position of having to perform multiple roles, each crucial to the Project’s success. While the Project was highly successful, met all its technical requirements, and provided critical aerothermodynamics data for the Orion Launch Abort System (LAS), its success can in part be attributed to the surge efforts of the PM and other key members.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. The PM’s performance, as a multi-rolled, lean team member and leader, and hence the Project’s success were aided by:
    1. Having experience in each of the roles he was performing
    2. A seasoned team that did not require much PM oversight
    3. A well understood set of roles and responsibilities to eliminate task drop outs
    4. A streamlined set of project processes (i.e., reduced reporting)
    5. A highly communicative and trusting team environment
    6. Access to “as-needed” expertise in the areas he was covering
  2. Projects that are extremely streamlined are dependent, to an unknown degree, on the personal surge capacity of its team members
    1. This is a personal characteristic that is very difficult to measure or estimate
    2. Past history may be an indicator of an individual’s capability and desire to occasionally put in surge efforts to meet work objectives

Recommendation(s)

  1. Project members that are required to fulfill multiple roles should be provided the aids described in number one above
  2. Streamlined projects should be aware that its personnel may require some surge capability in order to meet its objectives
    1. When selecting project members, their past surge efforts and current living circumstances should be taken into account
    2. Projects should prepare to support surge efforts with:
      1. As needed external help
      2. Personnel recognition (time-off and monetary awards)
      3. Relaxation and relief of reporting requirements
