Driving Event / Description

Figure 1: An artist's concept of the proposed Asteroid Redirect Mission

The event was conducted by ARM Program Director Michele Gates, and the lessons learned discussion was facilitated by Human Exploration and Operations Mission Directorate (HEOMD) Chief Knowledge Officer Patrick Johnson. It is important to note that multiple participants in the ARM program have developed detailed lessons learned reports that provide extensive contextual details associated with various ARM implementation issues at JSC, LaRC, GRC, and JPL. The ARM Program was made up of two projects: the Asteroid Redirect Robotic Mission (ARRM) followed by the Asteroid Redirect Crew Mission (ARCM).

 

 

Lesson(s) Learned

The lessons learned discussion framework was composed of four areas: technical, programmatic, external engagement, and “miscellaneous,” a catch-all bin that included IT, calendar integration, and political topics. Some notable issues within the technical discussion included:

  • Uncertainty in key mission requirements: Launch vehicle uncertainty that arose due to not being able to make a launch vehicle selection in Phase A created problems in the Asteroid Redirect Robotic Mission (ARRM) project, affecting design options and working baselines, and impacting mass, margin, and other fundamental design issues.
  • The ARM Program also faced uncertainty with respect to future crew operations factors (e.g., crew size, extravehicular activity from airlock), which impacted the ability to mature plans and develop requirements.
  • Shift in requirements paradigm: A major issue in moving from Key Decision Point (KDP)-A to KDP-B was the shift from a “capabilities-driven” mission to a “requirements-driven” mission. The decision was made to capture a larger asteroid than originally planned, which exceeded the existing baseline capabilities. The effect of this shift was not recognized by key stakeholders.
  • Uncertainty in the asteroid properties drove the ARM Capture Module (CAPM) robustness study. In response to the trade study for CAPM robustness, ARRM management prepared changes to the Level 1 requirements consistent with the trade study but was unable to finish that process with HQ.

Recommendation(s)

Discussions throughout the meeting returned to the topics of defining and managing requirements. The principal takeaway is the importance of “up-front and early-on” engagement with the technical authorities (Safety and Mission Assurance (SMA) and engineering communities) to establish mission requirements, to the extent possible and maintain continuity as requirements evolve. It was also known that the changes were needed in the Level 1 requirements would help simplify the baseline design.
