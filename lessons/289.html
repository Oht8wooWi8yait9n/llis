NASA Lesson #0289: Magellan In-Flight Solar Array Deployment Indication

Lesson ID: #0289 (Database ID: 289)
Date: 1993-07-13
Organization: Jet Propulsion Laboratory
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

During the launch sequence of the Magellan spacecraft, the two solar panels were observed by the space shuttle astronauts to have deployed, but the "panels-latched" telemetry indication was not transmitted. The deployment mechanism Failure Mode Effects and Criticality Analysis (FMECA) had identified the potential for latching indication problems, therefore in the planned launch sequence the panels were rotated such that the subsequent Inertial Upper Stage (IUS) burn would drive the panels toward the latched position.

Analysis has shown that the anomaly was due to the combination of marginal microswitch actuation stroke and zero-g effects on the solar array hinge mechanism. This combination caused one or both of the series-wired microswitches to fail to close. During the IUS burn, a small shift of the panels resulted in microswitch closure and provided the proper telemetry indication.

Except for the time spent addressing the initial concern, the anomaly did not impact the Magellan mission.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Microswitches on Magellan were mounted such that they would detect the position of the solar panels (as opposed to the status of the latching mechanism). As a result, they were just a position indicator and not a "panels latched" indicator.
  2. A marginal microswitch actuation stroke plus zero-g effects can result in failure of the microswitch to close.
  3. Failure Mode Effects and Criticality Analysis (FMECA) is a worthwhile activity and can result in problem workarounds being built into flight sequences ahead of time.

Recommendation(s)

  1. Care should be taken in deciding where to mount telemetry transducers to assure that the desired function is actually being measured, and not just a related function.
  2. All applications of microswitches should take into account the need for overtravel in the actuation stroke.
  3. Perform a Failure Mode Effects and Criticality Analysis (FMECA) early in the spacecraft life cycle, and involve the cognizant design personnel.
