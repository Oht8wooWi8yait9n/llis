NASA Lesson #1284: Marketing Project Contributions, Successes and Research Results to Customers and Management

Lesson ID: #1284 (Database ID: 1284)
Date: 2002-01-01
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Despite the importance of the Project's research results to its industry and military customers, it had little visibility or advocacy within the NASA community. This environment may have contributed to budget cuts resulting in milestone de-scoping and cancellations of scheduled reviews the Project suffered. In addition to a lack of success in promoting the Project to NASA Senior Management, the Project had difficulty sharing information rapidly and effectively with its out-of-Agency partners and customers. At the time the Project and its parent Program were suddenly and unexpectedly cancelled, a web site was created to summarize the Program and Project's research results, contributions and successes. The web site became a much-needed and used communication vehicle between the Project and its customers, and in addition it documented Project achievements in formats convenient to present to Senior Management. Several users suggested that such a web site should have been mounted at the beginning of the project instead of at the end in order to facilitate rapid communication to all the interested parties.

Lesson(s) Learned

Lack of project promotion to upper management and inadequate or slow communication of project results can result in an inaccurate appraisal of a project's value to its customers. High visibility and timely communication increase a project's opportunities for recognition and gaining/maintaining support. Low visibility and slow communication may increase a project's vulnerability to budget cuts, milestone de-scoping and a perception that the research results are unimportant or too incremental to merit continued support.

Recommendation(s)

In order to enhance project support, ensure that advocacy, promotion, and knowledge dissemination are included in the project plan. Mechanisms and schedules for accomplishing these functions should take into account the diversity and requirements of each audience. A web site that serves as a project archive and showcase could be used to integrate and disseminate the needed information effectively, conveniently, and compatibly with the audience's requirements. Project management could also use various venues (i.e., open house activities, professional colloquia, and public outreach) in addition to standard reviews and presentations to ensure the Project's merits are recognized.
