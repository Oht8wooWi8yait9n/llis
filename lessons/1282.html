NASA Lesson #1282: Project Management: Changing Workforce Requirements

Lesson ID: #1282 (Database ID: 1282)
Date: 2002-01-01
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The Project underwent several transitions requiring a refocusing or resizing of project personnel resources. Thus, several employees were trimmed from the work force, causing a deflation in morale among the remaining Project personnel, in addition to the loss in capability. In response, the Project/Program management actively sought to place the trimmed workforce with other employment, either throughout the Program's other projects or with external sources. Knowing that their welfare was being protected and advocated for, the Project employees were able to sustain their productivity and morale.

Lesson(s) Learned

During Project downsizing and/or redirecting, care must be taken to allow people to adjust. This may mean finding employees alternate work so that they are not caught off guard, demoralized, or unsupported. This philosophy strengthens each remaining team member's commitment to the team and to management, increases morale, reduces anxiety, and maintains productivity.

Recommendation(s)

Project management must help employees secure alternative employment if necessary. Hiring contacts both within and external to the Program should be identified and maintained throughout the Project's lifecycle. These resources may even be included in the Project's risk management plan such that employee confidence is increased.

People are our greatest resource. While performance based contracts ignore the individual, we should not lose sight of the relationship between people's productivity and their treatment.
