Driving Event / Description

The SPST concluded that, although the Space Shuttle is a highly successful program, it has many shortfalls related to its original requirements, objectives, and goals. The team concluded that the Space Shuttle Program's efforts to control LCC have been inadequate and ineffective. During the Space Shuttle's development, the only major objective that was controlled with the use of a structured Engineering Management process was performance closure by managing flight systems weight. Objectives were set for LCC for the Shuttle, but no Engineering Management processes were exercised to provide control.

Lesson(s) Learned

The SPST stated that a major lesson learned from their studies was that much-improved, innovative processes must be developed and rigorously applied to effectively control life cycle cost. The SPST also stated that NASA must balance the Safety, Reliability, and Maintainability requirements to provide controls on the burden of recurring maintenance, thus providing operational effectiveness and LCC. Finally, the SPST concluded that another major lessons learned was that systems requirements must be clearly defined, flowed down, and controlled during the design development and test evaluation phases of the program. The requirements must cover all major objectives, not just the performance objectives but also the operational requirements for the system to achieve sustainable life cycle costs.

Recommendation(s)

The SPST recommends that NASA consider adopting the proven methods of controlling weight and performance and applying them to controlling the LCC. The SPST also recommends that a process be applied for balancing Safety, Reliability, and Maintainability requirements to provide controls on the Program’s recurring maintenance burden to provide operational effectiveness and LCC. In addition, the future programs need a complete functional systems breakdown structure of all activities to provide the total visibility of the entire task. This functional systems breakdown structure will allow the systems engineering activities to totally integrate each discipline and to optimize these activities at the total system level and not at the subsystem level. An approach to this system-level optimization is defined in the SPST report titled, "Space Transportation Systems Life Cycle Cost Assessment and Control," revised March 5, 2005.
