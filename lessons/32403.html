NASA Lesson #32403: Improved Operations and Backlog Management of NANADARTS at GRC

Lesson ID: #32403 (Database ID: 32403)
Date: 2023-04-27
Organization: Glenn Research Center
Program Phase: Not Specified
Topic / Category: Safety and Mission Assurance, Administration / Organization
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Per NPR 8735.1, NASA programs and projects must implement closed-loop reporting via NANADARTS whenever a GIDEP notice or NASA advisory may impact “critical work” as defined in NPD 8730.5. Therefore, all programs or projects that perform critical work must participate.

NASA has an agency GIDEP/NANADARTS coordinator, and each center has two center GIDEP/NANADARTS coordinators. The agency GIDEP/NANADARTS coordinator and their team are responsible for extracting any new NASA-relevant GIDEP notices from GIDEP, and along with any newly released NASA Advisories, distributing these via the NANADARTS website to each of the center GIDEP/NANADARTS coordinators. The center coordinators are responsible for distributing these, again via NANADARTS, to the participating programs and projects. These programs and projects will have a dedicated required responder who is responsible for receiving and evaluating these GIDEP notices and NASA advisories via NANADARTS. These documents are evaluated for usage by the required responders within the NANADARTS system and this information is then reported out to the agency.

Unfortunately, due to the causes described below, and not necessarily the fault of any one person, unsent and unanswered documents began accumulating both on the coordinators’ end, and the required responders’ end, which ultimately led to a dangerously large backlog.

 

  1. Distribution of documents from the agency coordinator to the center coordinators is irregular. Sometimes a hundred new documents may be sent from the agency coordinator to the center coordinators in a day. Sometimes weeks will go by with zero new documents. This is not necessarily the fault of anyone. This is determined by external factors – mainly the rate at which new GIDEP notices have been submitted to GIDEP by other agencies and companies, and how many of those are relevant to NASA. This has the effect of making it difficult both for center coordinators and required responders to develop a regular routine.
  2. The center coordinators at GRC do not have any assigned time to perform NANADARTS duties, and there exists no WBS, which leads to assigned work taking precedence over NANADARTS.
  3. Center-wide there has been a history of inadequate awareness levels and somewhat low priority given to NANADARTS activities. For example, most GRC employees have never heard of NANADARTS and are unaware of how it relates to GIDEP, nor does there exist much familiarity with either the NPR or GRC work instruction that discusses it. Also, it was often not well-known across the center which projects needed to participate, how to participate, why it’s important, and what would be the potential consequences of not participating. Consequently, NANADARTS responsibilities in the past have often been given very low priority.

This significant backlog was noted as a major finding in an external audit.

Lesson(s) Learned

Several steps were taken to successfully eliminate the backlog:

1. As a result of the external audit finding, center coordinators, branch chiefs, and upper management decided on giving the NANADARTS process a higher priority and greater visibility by having center coordinators report status updates of backlog counts at the monthly Safety & Mission Assurance Management Board meetings. Management from across the center attends this meeting and was made aware of the seriousness of this issue through these out-briefs. This action continued until the backlog was eliminated.

2. To deal with the irregular pacing of incoming alerts and notices, to increase communication between everyone involved, and to promote a greater sense of importance to this task, center coordinators developed a more regular routine for the benefit of both themselves and the required responders. Center coordinators did this by utilizing NANADARTS user tools to compile backlog counts at the start of every month, and then emailing these numbers as monthly status updates to each required responder and their project manager. The required responders and Project Managers were also reminded that these backlog counts were being reported up the chain at the monthly S&MA Management Board meetings, as described above.

3. Center coordinators increased awareness of the NANADARTS process:

a. Center coordinators gave presentations to individual codes on the NANADARTS process so that the Program/Project Managers could better understand the process, the importance of it, and to determine whether their program or project should be participating.

b. Center coordinators were able to add a link to the NANADARTS website on GRC’s one-stop-shop front page, “Inside Glenn,” under the “Employee Tools” dropdown. (https://nasa.sharepoint.com/sites/grc)

c. Center coordinators hosted a booth for GIDEP/NANADARTS activities at GRC’s annual Safety Day and will continue to do so at future safety days.

4. GRC center coordinators reached out to other center’s coordinators for guidance:

a. Other center’s coordinators provided guidance on how they conducted NANADARTS training to required responders and project managers. The GRC center coordinators incorporated some of the other centers’ training documentation into their own to improve their training process. Once GRC’s training materials were updated, training sessions were held with all required responders and their project managers.

b. Other center’s coordinators also provided their centers’ work instructions on NANADARTS. The GRC center coordinators took guidance from these documents and published a new revision of the center’s work instruction offering finer details and more direction.

5. Center coordinators invited all other NASA center coordinators to attend a face-to-face event at GRC to discuss other ways to improve how NANADARTS is implemented both center-wide and agency-wide.

Recommendation(s)

  1. There should be center-wide awareness of NANADARTS. Programs and projects should be aware of whether they should be participating, how they participate, why it’s important, and what the potential consequences are of not participating.
  2. NANADARTS documents should be distributed by the center coordinators on a regular basis to the appropriate required responders. Then, these documents should be responded to in a timely manner by those required responders

Additional steps that could be taken:

  • The center coordinators at GRC do not have any assigned time to perform NANADARTS duties, and there exists no WBS, which leads to assigned work taking precedence over NANADARTS.
  • Additionally, coordinators are having to charge time spent on NANADARTS activities to their projects. Most other NASA centers have funding for their center coordinators, while GRC does not.

Evidence / Additional Context

Implementing these steps successfully eliminated the massive backlog in less than 6 months and have helped to maintain low numbers afterwards.
