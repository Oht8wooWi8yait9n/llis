NASA Lesson #1439: Accident Investigations/Contingency Action Plan/Personnel Planning

Lesson ID: #1439 (Database ID: 1439)
Date: 2003-08-31
Organization: Johnson Space Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Personnel requirements for the NASA Task Force were unknown until accident occurred.

Lesson(s) Learned

Basic personnel requirements for support to the NASA Task Force should be identified ahead of time at each NASA center.

Recommendation(s)

  • Identify and update basic personnel requirements in each Center's emergency action plan at least annually. For this Task Force, the “application/volunteer process” worked well to identify potential candidates after the fact. The Task Force director should have immediate access to this personnel database. The following recommended guidelines can assist the Task Force Director in selecting technical and professional personnel:
    • Process-oriented
    • Self-Initiators requiring little or no direction to accomplish task
    • "Big Picture" individuals who pay attention to detail, but don't get stuck there.
    • Can commit 24/7 time and energy through "start-up phase" (~1 to 2 months)
    • Problem solvers that may not know all the answers, but knows how to get them
    • "Whatever-it-takes attitude"
    • No personnel (civil service or contractor) who were involved in day-to-day shuttle operations or management of the activity in progress when the accident happened.
  • Staff up immediately. Part of the stress early on was based on an overwhelming amount of work, and not enough people to do it. People should be pre-identified and known to be on-call. Preplanning can determine the number of people (at different phases in the task), and types of expertise that are required.
  • Assign Contingency Response Teams of technical, operational and administrative personnel at all operational Centers. Stagger assigned duties to respond to major and minor incidents.
  • Make team building and training part of this permanent team.
