Driving Event / Description

JWST had a prolonged technology development period following the selection of the prime contractor in 2002. It is difficult to predict how long it will take to develop a new technology, and during the period from 2002 to 2008, JWST struggled with several cutting-edge developments. These developments took longer, and consequently cost more, than forecast during formulation. Since commitment, telescope development has faced continual budget problems. An independent review panel assessing the JWST Program in 2010 concluded that the problems causing cost growth and schedule delays were associated with budgeting and program management, not technical performance, as the budget baseline accepted at the confirmation review did not reflect the most probable cost with adequate reserves in each year of project execution. This resulted in a project that was simply not executable within the budgeted resources.

Lesson(s) Learned

Once the program was put on a sound foundation for development through launch with the JWST rebaseline in 2011, NASA had to address the management issues identified by the independent review panel. The new JWST Program Director describes changes in five areas that have led, thus far, to considerably improved telescope development performance. The areas are:
  • clearly defined authority, responsibility and accountability;
  • improved communication;
  • commitment to the established baseline;
  • improved risk management;
  • independent review and increased program planning and control capability and analysis.

Recommendation(s)

Projects experience challenges that are not limited to pushing technology to the limits or using existing technologies in entirely new ways. When replanning or rebaselining a project, consider program and project management and how it is helping the project to succeed or may be causing it to fail. In the case of the James Webb Space Telescope, it took management changes at several levels to finally put the project on a solid foundation -- the changes went beyond putting new people in place and included taking new approaches to Program/project management. Success for this successor to the Hubble Space Telescope may ultimately be due to a combination of technical excellence and a new management approach.
