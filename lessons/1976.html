NASA Lesson #1976: Creation of a National Aeronautics Operational Monitoring System Research Data Center

Driving Event / Description

Because there was intense public and Congressional interest in the NAOMS data, NASA promised the Congress initial release of data (called phase 1) by the end of 2007. In a memorandum dated November 19, 2007, the NASA administrator directed the Chief of the Office of Safety and Mission Assurance to lead an effort to release as much NAOMS survey information as possible by the end of 2007. The task was executed soon thereafter with the initial posting of NAOMS information on the NASA website on December 31, 2007. Phase 1 was completed on February 6, 2008, with a more comprehensive web posting of NAOMS information. Some people and organizations outside of NASA have said that the necessary data redaction for Phase 1 was overly conservative, and that more data could have been released.

After the Phase 1 postings, NASA initiated another review of all the NAOMS survey responses in order to allow for further release of information. A project team was selected to implement the recommendation from the NASA Information Release Advisory Panel (2008) relating to an additional release of NAOMS Phase 1 information by the end of the year (2008). This effort is referred to as Phase 2 of the NAOMS information release.

The objective of the NAOMS Phase 2 information release was to redact the raw NAOMS survey responses using a redaction release strategy that:
  • Releases the maximum amount of survey information, but
  • Does not release commercial confidential information
  • Meets NASAs obligation for survey response participant confidentiality as defined by the NAOMS Information Release Advisory Panel (2008)
  • Minimizes the threat to participant anonymity

NASA intended to protect pilot participant identity and never planned to publish NAOMS raw data. NASA had to engage in an optimization process by releasing as much of the NAOMS data as possible, yet still maintain the anonymity and confidentiality of the pilots who were the participants in the survey (Phase 2 data).

In retrospect, the NAOMS data release project required extensive efforts to release phase 2 data. These included: planning, redaction, quality assurance, risk analysis, and validation efforts. The cost of NAOMS phase 2 was approximately $1.1M.

NASA did not attempt to implement a Research Data Center during NAOMS Phase 2 project, because the after-the-fact involvement of a statistical agency governed by CIPSEA could not confer the legal protections afforded by CISPEA on NAOMS data. On September 30, 2008, NASA released Phase 2 data within cost, schedule, and technical requirements and provided anonymity to those taking part in the Phase 1 survey.

Lesson(s) Learned

Most statistical agencies (e.g. Census Bureau) are governed by the Confidentiality Information Protection and Statistical Efficiency Act (CIPSEA) of 2002 when releasing confidential information is at issue. CIPSEA requires all federal agencies to protect data or information acquired by the agency under a pledge of confidentiality for exclusively statistical purposes from being disclosed in identifiable form. The NASA Office of the General Counsel determined that CIPSEA does not apply to the NAOMS information collection. For purposes of CIPSEA, NASA is considered a non-statistical agency. As a non-statistical agency, the information collection must be conducted by civil servants to have CIPSEA apply. During the NAOMS project, contractors collected survey information. Also, in order to have CIPSEA protection, there is a specific "confidentiality pledge" that comes directly from the statute. This pledge was not read to the NAOMS participants. The bottom-line is that CIPSEA does not apply to NAOMS. Also, allowing another statistical agency, who has CIPSEA coverage, to archive and distribute the raw data to qualified identities, will not further protect the NAOMS data.

The very best approach to an aviation survey and data base project would be to have it conducted by a statistical agency of the US Government. This would also allow the establishment of a Research Data Center under the strict control of the statistical agency. Under such rules, even the raw survey data could be used by researchers without any redaction. The research would have to be performed by the researchers in Washington DC, on the premises of the statistical agency and under their rules, constraints, and supervision. No expensive redaction project would be needed and confidentiality could be assured.

The concept of a Research Data Center is a means to secure facilities designed for outside researchers to access confidential agency data. Several statistical federal agencies (e.g. Census Bureau) have instituted the concept of a Research Data Center with much success. Access to the NAOMS raw survey responses would be under defined criteria and confidentiality conditions. The Research Data Center could be established within NASA or through sponsorship from a statistical agency already equipped with the infrastructure to share, control, and monitor the use of confidential survey responses.

Under the concept of a Research Data Center, an interested party would submit a request or proposal to NASA for the use of NAOMS raw survey responses. The proposal would be evaluated for merit, NASA mission related interests, and the requesters ability to maintain confidentiality. Upon acceptance of the proposal, the requester would agree to the terms of use of the raw survey responses, including the confidentiality requirements. Any products created with, or from, the use of the raw survey responses would be provided to NASA before its publication and release. The Research Data Center provides the framework to allow individuals or organizations with proven capabilities to analyze the NAOMS survey responses with minimal cost to NASA.

Additonal NAOMS information including history, memorandums, methodology and press releases can be found by entering "NAOMS" in the NASA Headquarters home page search box at http://www.nasa.gov/centers/hq/home/index.html. Additional information on the Confidentiality Information Protection and Statistical Efficiency Act of 2002 can be found by contacting government statistical agencies such as the Census Bureau.

Recommendation(s)

Prior to conducting similar NAOMS survey in the future, NASA should consider collecting data in a way that makes it available for the public to view and use raw data through a Research Data Center with CIPSEA coverage. Establishment of a Research Data Center under the strict control of a statistical agency would have the legal authority granted by the United States government to protect the disclosure of the data and release raw data, fully or in part, through controlled means. The public would perform raw data research at the Research Data Center, on the premises of the statistical agency and under their rules, constraints, and supervision. Expensive redaction projects such as NAOMS Phase 2 may not be necessary and survey confidentiality will be assured.
