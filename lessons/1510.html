NASA Lesson #1510: Orbital Space Plane - Operational Design Process

Lesson ID: #1510 (Database ID: 1510)
Date: 2003-07-01
Organization: Marshall Space Flight Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

An operational focus was applied by aligning operations equivalent to engineering in the organization and keeping flight, ground, and integrated operations engaged throughout the development.

Lesson(s) Learned

“A user will tell you anything you ask about-and nothing more.” So, OSP asked many questions, asked early, and asked often. An operational focus was applied by aligning operations equivalent to engineering in the organization and keeping flight, ground, and integrated operations engaged throughout the development.

Additionally:

a. Integrated Operations. There was recognition that the ground system must be worked in an integrated fashion with the flight system. The operations community was included in the development process from the start and direction was provided by the program manager to optimize the total system versus the vehicle.

b. Operators in the Acquisition. Operational conscience extends into SEB membership and all other aspects of the procurement process. The OSP SEB Voting Board members included representatives of the design and operations centers giving a more balanced experience base and perspective in procuring a better system. Committee structure included an Operations Committee with representation from the astronaut corps, the flight controllers from JSC and the ground operations personnel from KSC.

c. Focus on Operability. Early in the formulation phase, the OSP Program committed to placing a high emphasis on ensuring “operability” in the design, to reduce life cycle costs and reduce process-induced failures. When pressure was ramping up, the emphasis on operations infusion into the design fell by the wayside.

Recommendation(s)

Systems operations must be planned for up front and forced into the design and facilitization. The operations community should be included in the development process from the start and direction should be provided by the program manager to optimize the total system versus the vehicle. The Design committees should be well balanced with vehicle design and operations engineering support to assure a balance between performance, operability and maintainability of the end product.
