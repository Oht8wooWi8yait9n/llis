NASA Lesson #5476: The Value of Post Flight Reports and System Reviews in Understanding and Correcting Space Craft Anomalies

Lesson ID: #5476 (Database ID: 5476)
Date: 2010-06-03
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Implementation
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Review boards, Program level review processes, Maintenance, Product Assurance, Reliability, Review systems and boards, Engineering design and project processes and standards

Driving Event / Description

During the Space Shuttle Program, it was common to lose, or partially lose, a fill material used to fill gaps between thermal protection system tiles (Ames Gap fillers). Other common issues found post flight were charred filler bar locations and missing putty repairs used to fix chips in tiles. A post flight report was published, but one flight was never really compared to the previous ones, so these issues were not immediately apparent. Post flight reports and system review presentations were archived for historical data trending purposes.

Figure 1: Charred Filler Bar Locations

Lesson(s) Learned

After compiling post flight inspection reports and adding a detailed systems review conducted by the Chief Engineer, the NASA community can correlate space vehicle performance to the effects to flight hardware. Data can be used to identify these common issues that continually reoccur, so that they can be addressed and eliminated. Post flight reviews also help to understand aerothermal effects on space hardware and validate existing analysis data.

Recommendation(s)

For any future space flight vehicle or significant program or project, a requirement should be established to formally conduct system reviews and post flight, or post milestone, reports. The requirement should establish cataloging and archiving of reports for historical data trending purposes, using the Space Shuttle System as a model.
