NASA Lesson #1406: Need for Increased Use of Formal Engineering Reports

Lesson ID: #1406 (Database ID: 1406)
Date: 2004-05-06
Organization: Langley Research Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The NESC performs independent testing and analyses of technical issues. Reliable and credible data must be collected from the programs and projects in order to perform independent and expert technical analyses. In several cases the NESC has found it difficult to collect the data in a reliable and credible format.

Lesson(s) Learned

Much of the technical data collected by the NESC typically comes in the form of Power Point charts that attempt to convey the results of an engineering study. Many times the charts are the only documentation of an engineering study or analysis. Technical data in this format can be plagued with problems of misunderstanding, unintentional misrepresentation and inconsistency as was pointed out in the Columbia Accident Investigation Report (Volume 1 Pg.182, para. 7.3 and pg.191), and confirmed by the NESC experience contained in this Lesson Learned.

The lack of disciplined, formally documented technical information increases the difficulty of performing an expert independent analysis or evaluation in a timely manner. The data/information can be lost forever if not properly captured at the time of the analysis.
 

Recommendation(s)

Agency engineering organizations should use formal technical reports to document the results of engineering studies and analyses. Undoubtedly emphasis should always be on content regardless of whether PowerPoint or an engineering report is used for communication.

Engineering studies and analyses should be documented in the NASA TM format. Journal paper formats, e.g., American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (AIAA), should be acceptable for reports when time is of the essence. These formats help to reinforce the discipline and rigor needed to develop reliable and credible data.

PowerPoint is a tool that should be limited in use to top-level presentations including status reporting and briefings, and should include a disclaimer that the charts are for status only.
