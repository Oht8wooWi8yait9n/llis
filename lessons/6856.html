NASA Lesson #6856: Closed-Loop Infusion of Lessons Learned into Center Procedures & Training

Driving Event / Description

NASA has an established process (Reference (1)) for documenting and disseminating lessons learned from programs and projects, including those that develop and operate very high unit value spacecraft systems and support equipment. The NASA Lesson Learned Information System (LLIS) is a searchable repository of lessons learned contributed by NASA HQ and the NASA Centers. However, independent assessments of the NASA lessons learned process conducted in 2002, 2003, and 2011 (References (2), (3), and (4)), have concluded that, in most cases, NASA programs and projects need to do more to heed and apply these lessons learned.

To assure that NASA lesson learned recommendations were fully considered during the appropriate phases of the project lifecycle, major projects at the NASA/Caltech Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) conducted self-assessments of project conformance with the recommendations. At major project milestones, subject matter experts were assigned to determine each recommendation's applicability to the mission, assess the potential mission impact of non-conformance, evaluate the current conformance status of the project, and propose a course of action. For example, on the Mars Exploration Rover (MER) lessons learned compliance matrix, one row listed a 2000 lesson learned contributed by NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (LLIS No. 0979 at https://llis.nasa.gov) that warned of the danger of in-flight thruster plume impingement on the spacecraft structure; the column documenting the MER response pointed to three-dimensional modeling performed by the project to eliminate this risk. However, this self-assessment practice is quite labor intensive for projects, and it does not assure that the appropriate person applies a project-relevant lesson learned at the optimal point in the project lifecycle. Hence, JPL decided to quotinfusequot lessons learned into JPL procedures and training at the institutional level such that JPL need not wholly depend on a project staff member making timely use of a lesson learned. The continuing practice of self-assessment aids in alerting projects to new lessons that have yet to be infused.

Early attempts to implement this lessons learned infusion process at JPL were unsuccessful due to the scope and complexity of incorporating lessons learned into JPL processes. Lessons learned were categorized to match the organizational units in the JPL line organization. For example, LLIS content related to spacecraft propulsion was forwarded to the manager of the JPL propulsion engineering section for incorporation into propulsion engineering procedures and training that apply to all JPL projects. To track the action item to completion, a JPL Preventive Action Notice (PAN) was issued against the section manager. There were several problems with this initial approach:

  1. There are thousands of JPL procedures and work instructions to which lesson learned recommendations could potentially apply.

  2. It was difficult to incorporate recommendations (i.e., advisory statements) into a mix of guidelines and requirements documents at the section level, and these are not standard requirements documents against which projects track compliance.

  3. The PANs were assigned to a process owner in the line organization, whereas JPL lessons learned process requirements are imposed primarily on the JPL project. Also, the lessons learned recommendations were not clearly actionable as their content was informational.

After these early lessons learned infusion efforts bogged down, a new development at JPL provided a cleaner method of infusion. Two JPL documents-- the JPL Design Principles (Reference (5)) and the JPL Flight Project Practices (Reference (6)), were re-issued as core engineering standards against which each JPL project is audited for compliance. Basically, the Design Principles (DP) document lists the minimum set of design requirements for all spaceflight systems, while the Flight Project Practices (FPP) covers the mission engineering activities other than design engineering (e.g., testing, problem reporting, system safety, quality assurance). Reviewing this compendium of quotthings JPL projects should always do,quot it is apparent that the lesson learned recommendations are written perhaps one level of detail lower, such that they provide historical examples that reinforce the DP and FPP content. Hence, lessons learned infusion into the DP and FPP provides two benefits:

  1. Only two standards documents provide effective closed-loop infusion of lessons learned, and

  2. Lessons learned cross-referenced to the DP and FPP paragraphs provide additional clarification, rationale, and justification for the requirements in these core standards, and provide insight into the risks of noncompliance. This helps project management and system contractors to understand the requirements and avoid unnecessary DP/FPP waiver requests.

The JPL Office of the Chief Engineer took responsibility for drafting a matrix cross-referencing paragraphs in the DP and FPP with the corresponding lessons learned, including JPL and relevant NASA Center lessons learned in the LLIS. For example, a DP requirement relating to single failure tolerance was cross-referenced to LLIS Nos. 1835, 1806, 1743, and 1733. To validate the proposed cross-references, the JPL Chief Engineer directed the JPL Engineering Board (JEB) to vet them. The JEB validation was completed in 2011, and the 2012 revisions of the DP (Rev. 6) and the FPP (Rev. 9) now feature hot links placed beneath individual DP/FPP paragraphs pointing to the corresponding lesson(s) learned.

This closed-loop resolution of lesson learned recommendations does not depend on the initiative of project personnel to read the DP and FPP documents. Instead, each JPL project is already required to complete a DP compliance matrix (Reference (7)) and an FPP compliance matrix (Reference (8)) and check-off project compliance with each requirement.

Lesson(s) Learned

Independent assessments of the NASA lessons learned process have reported that NASA programs and projects fail to systematically make use of the NASA lessons learned repository. Although major JPL projects do conduct self-assessments of project compliance with the lesson learned recommendations, it is difficult to assure that the appropriate subject matter expert applies a key lesson learned at the specific point in time best suited to lesson learning by a project. However, self-assessments can be augmented by active measures at the institutional level to infuse NASA lessons learned into key engineering standards and processes.

Recommendation(s)

To ensure that lesson learned recommendations in the NASA lessons learned repository are used by NASA programs and projects, infuse the recommendations into the key NASA Center standards and processes by which work is always done.
