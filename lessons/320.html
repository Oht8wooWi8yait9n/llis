NASA Lesson #0320: Crew Training for On-Orbit Operation of Payload Equipment

Lesson ID: #0320 (Database ID: 320)
Date: 1993-08-04
Organization: Marshall Space Flight Center
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Experience on SL-1 indicated that efficiency of orbital operations was somewhat compromised when ground training did not include exposure to high-fidelity training simulators.

Lesson(s) Learned

Training and mission simulation for crew and ground support personnel relating to on-orbit payload operation should be continued on future missions to enhance the efficiency of on-orbit operations. The training simulators and mission simulations should be of high fidelity. The number and duration of mission simulations should be based upon the length and complexity of the mission. A minimum of 2 simulations 3 to 4 weeks apart, one to include science team members, should be required.

Recommendation(s)

Provide crew training which encompasses the following concepts: high-fidelity mockups available in the crew training center; the degree of fidelity should be driven by the degree of safety concern and the amount of crew time dedicated to an experiment during the mission. High-fidelity software simulators available for all experiments, which have crew interface or data output. The degree of fidelity should be driven by safety concerns and on-orbit crew time scheduled for the experiment. Provisions for hands-on crew participation in integrated tests. At least two long-duration mission simulations should be conducted with mandatory participation of the science team (principal investigator) in at least one.

Evidence / Additional Context

Comparison of operations of equipment with training accomplished on high-fidelity mockups with operation of equipment for which good mockups were not available demonstrated the effectiveness of the recommended approach. Subsequent missions have supported this conclusion.
