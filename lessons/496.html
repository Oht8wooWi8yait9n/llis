NASA Lesson #0496: Improper Welded Joints in Rocket Engine Combustion Chamber (1995)

Lesson ID: #0496 (Database ID: 496)
Date: 1997-03-13
Organization: Jet Propulsion Laboratory
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

In the first test firing of a Mars Global Surveyor (MGS) main engine, the engine was destroyed within a few seconds. The combustion chamber had exceeded the maximum allowable temperature, causing parts to melt and rendering the engine unusable. Internal examination with a boroscope revealed an annular extrusion in the combustion chamber due to overpenetration of weld material in the joint between the injector and the chamber.

The MGS main engine injector fits over the head end of the combustion chamber; the two parts are attached with a weld about the circumference of an overlapping joint. Vendor use of an incorrect weld schedule produced an annular ridge, which interfered with the flow of boundary layer cooling fuel within the chamber. This disturbed flow resulted in an untenable thermal condition.

A boroscopic examination of the chamber prior to the test firing would have revealed the defect.

Lesson(s) Learned

Conduct a thorough external and internal inspection of rocket engine combustion chambers, paying particular attention to welded joints, prior to hot fire testing.

Evidence / Additional Context


JPL has referenced this lesson learned as additional rationale and guidance supporting Paragraph 7.3.3 ("Safety and Mission Assurance Practices: Quality Assurance") in the Jet Propulsion Laboratory standard "Flight Project Practices, Rev. 7," JPL DocID 58032, September 30, 2008.
