NASA Lesson #33502: Failure Of Design Review Process To Adequately Validate Late-Stage Changes

Lesson ID: #33502 (Database ID: 33502)
Date: 2021-12-12
Organization: Goddard Space Flight Center
Program Phase: Implementation &raquo; Phase C
Topic / Category: EEE Parts, Spacecraft and Spacecraft Instruments, Configuration control and data management, Contractor relationships, Configuration Change Control
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

Wildcat Processor Card (WPC) #3 was damaged during work at a NASA/GSFC contractor facility tasked with conformal coating, component staking, and installation of two heat sinks on the fully populated printed circuit board. The damage occurred when the heatsinks were installed and torqued on top of a Processor and Field Programmable Gate Array (FPGA). The heatsinks and associated thermal material interfered with thermistors already installed on top of the two components. Both components were destroyed in the event, and WPC #3 was eventually downgraded to non-flight status after part replacement yielded additional concerns.

The requirement to add the heatsinks was determined after the initial drawing release for the WPC and was based on thermal analysis of the FPGA and Processor when operating at nominal power dissipations under hot operational conditions. Thermal-vacuum testing of engineering test unit cards showed positive thermal margin on the parts without the heatsinks, but they were added to the flight design as a precaution.

The addition of the heatsinks was communicated verbally and through emails, not through an official engineering change order process. The revisions were reviewed and approved in the project configuration management system using 2-dimensional drawings, but the interference between the heatsinks and thermistors on the two parts was not identified.

Members of the contractor engineering staff questioned the rationale of mounting the heatsinks in the manner they were being asked to implement and even held conversations with some members of management. But, due to the maturity of the flight board, everyone involved assumed that the WPC product development team had already calculated and approved the necessary clearances needed to attach the heat sinks without damaging the processors.

Lesson(s) Learned

  • Tight schedules can adversely impact review processes and important decision making.
  • All project personnel (civil servant & contractor) must be empowered to raise questions, express concerns, and point out anomalies.

Recommendation(s)

  1. Schedule pressure should not be a reason to avoid formal engineering review processes, even for what are considered a minor change.
  2. Project leadership should explicitly and continually express the need for everyone to bring concerns forward.
