NASA Lesson #0305: Galileo Retro Propulsion Module (RPM) Flow Decay Problem

Lesson ID: #0305 (Database ID: 305)
Date: 1993-09-01
Organization: Jet Propulsion Laboratory
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

When the Galileo launch date was delayed from 1986 to 1989 with an increase in mission duration from four to eight years, the RPM design was re-evaluated. It was determined that a significant risk existed of in-flight loss of propulsion capability over the extended mission due to oxidizer flow decay. Flow decay is due to blockage of small oxidizer flow passages and filters by the accumulation of contamination products caused by interaction between the stainless steel lines and components with the nitrogen tetroxide oxidizer.

Replacement of most of the stainless steel oxidizer feed system with titanium has alleviated the concern.

Lesson(s) Learned

Contaminants created by the interaction of stainless steel lines and components with nitrogen tetroxide oxidizer can result in the blockage of small oxidizer flow passages and filters.

Recommendation(s)

  1. The use of stainless steel components in combination with nitrogen tetroxide should be eliminated where obstruction in small passages may occur.
  2. Projects of long mission duration that use nitrogen tetroxide oxidizer systems and cannot avoid the use of stainless steel should develop a mechanism to predict flow decay hazard conditions.
  3. Oxidizers should be purified immediately before loading to minimize contamination products.

Evidence / Additional Context

JPL has referenced this lesson learned as additional rationale and guidance supporting Paragraph 5.14.1 ("Management Practices: Acquisition and Surveillance"), and Paragraph 6.10.1 ("Engineering Practices: Materials, Processes, and Contamination Control - Practices") in the Jet Propulsion Laboratory standard "Flight Project Practices, Rev. 7," JPL DocID 58032, September 30, 2008.
