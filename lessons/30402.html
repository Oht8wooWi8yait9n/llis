Driving Event / Description

In December 2020, a project to provide utility services at the Kennedy Space Center (KSC) was initiated. The Utility Energy Services Contract scope covered 22 facilities with mechanical, electrical, HVAC and water as well as street lighting and 2-megawatt (MW) solar fields. Lessons learned were collected in six major areas: Communication, Documentation, Project Requirements, Cost/ Finance, Organization and Technical Reviews. 

Lesson(s) Learned

The Lessons Learned are divided into six major categories:

 

Communication
  • Ensure Facility Managers are aware of scope and timeline
  • Keep stakeholders updated and aware of progress
  • Establish prime stakeholder meeting frequency
  • Utilize Gantt chart where possible to project path and communicate with stakeholders

Documentation
  • Compile the 1509 draft early on in the process
  • Ensure proper UESC project title
  • Compile Special Conditions document draft early
  • Ensure demo list is updated
  • Properly document legal requirements and the NASA process

Project Requirements
  • Determine speed needed as a larger scope tends to take much longer to accomplish

Cost/ Finance
  • Determine need for Basis of Estimate from contractor and acquire
  • Determine support costs for planning
  • Ensure finance terms and review Points of Contact (POC)s are defined and followed

Organization
  • Determine full POC list as soon as possible
  • Determine stakeholders and responsibilities early

Technical Reviews
  • Ensure review cycles are established
  • Determine need for Tech Eval

Procurement Officer may need to be delegated more responsibility

Cost/ Finance

Title

Driving Event

Lesson(s) Learned

Recommendation

Determine support costs for planning

Essential for budgeting, planning, and the PPBE cycle(s)

Know contingency and BOSS / NEMCON support activities and approximate cost

KSC estimates plan ~6% for SEIS/Shop support, ~2% Environmental, ~10% Contingency

Ensure Finance terms and review POCs are defined and followed

Prevents committal to non-ideal terms or higher expense to NASA over life of loan

Finance elements: have full list of requirements from lenders reviewed by finance and procurement prior to ESCO (FPL) engaging with lenders.

Establish a proper POC list of stakeholders for making a decision (KSC: finance, procurement, legal) and communicate timeline for decision need (5 business days)

Determine need for Basis of Estimate from contractor and acquire

For Sole Sourcing - "Certificate for Current Cost and Pricing," employees will work on job. If labor rates are less, CO can sign a "Certificate of Defective Pricing" asking for money back.

Need to get Basis of Estimate from contractor to save time in the long run

Determine need for Basis of Estimate from contractor and acquire

Communication

Title

Driving Event

Lesson(s) Learned

Recommendation

Keep stakeholders updated and aware of progress

Ensure scope is tracked to prevent duplication of work by other groups and keep customers/stakeholders aware of status

Customer engagement: continue to engage at every stage of UESC with each review cycle to maintain awareness

Beneficial to have all programs in same meeting vs separate meetings for each program

Ensure Facility Managers are aware of scope and timeline

Program managers may not be aware of information that Facility Managers have that could contribute to project success

Ensure every facility in scope has Facility Manager (FM) identified throughout process

Alt could be Program Manager if no FM is available; however, engage at beginning of Feasibility Study and at 100% at minimum

Utilize Gantt chart where possible to project path and communicate with stakeholders

Non-essential delays are incurred in determining next steps

Determine if potential parallel activities can be done

Scope compiled, Construction of Facilities (CoF) to complete actions, programs to review and approve

Establish prime stakeholder meeting frequency

Without constant communication with stakeholder groups, project tends to lose focus and momentum as well as ease in resolving problems that pop up

More frequent meetings with GG and programs/owners of facilities to avoid late game changes with programs

KSC has seen success in a half-hour weekly tag up on UESC

Project Requirements

Title

Driving Event

Lesson(s) Learned

Recommendation

Determine speed needed as larger scope tends to take much longer to accomplish

KSC's UESC Phase 1 Feasibility Study took ~2 years to accomplish due to significant size of scope and POC's involved

Benefit of having UESC limited to single program vs a multi-program effort

Split UESC based on scope type (i.e., Lighting) or by Program (i.e., ISS)

Compile Special Conditions document draft early

Special Conditions document discussions and decisions can take a decent time to complete; desire is to not have this as a long-lead event extending timeline

Special Conditions document to be started earlier -- at possibly 90% stage rather than Tech Eval

Seek template from EEP or prior center project template to copy and paste

Technical Reviews

Title

Driving Event

Lesson(s) Learned

Recommendation

Determine need for Tech Eval

Tech evaluations take time and can become challenging if long periods of time exist between review cycles

Potentially limit Tech Eval to once every ‘x’ months

Recommend having at 90%, 100%, and Final only

Ensure review cycles are established

Prevents extra, unnecessary reviews and potential additional incurred costs for Utility and/or government

Include in contract how many review cycles to include to avoid incurring an additional cycle

A 45%, 90%, 100%, Final, with no additional updates to Final submittal

Documentation

Title

Driving Event

Lesson(s) Learned

Recommendation

Compile 1509 draft early

1509 reviews can take up to 3 weeks, delaying project from moving forward if not planned properly

1509 actions – ensure payback verbiage is well documented and available for response

Meet with local center and NASA HQ POCs to ensure process and data are drafted correctly and reviewed, which would expedite final 1509/1510 submittal

Ensure proper UESC project title

From discussing the KSC named "UESC Phase 1" effort became confusing with discussion with NREL, NASA HQ, and CoF as "phase" can mean many different things

Project naming convention needs consistency and clarity as “Phases” tend to be confusing since the term can imply Feasibility Study vs Preliminary Assessment or others

Name effort based on project category or program (example: Lighting, ISS)

Ensure demo list is updated

Pursued scope in facility soon to be demonstrated

Saves time investing in facilities soon to be demolished

Engage with Facilities Division and Demo POC during each phase of UESC

Properly document legal requirements and NASA process

Without prepared response to common questions, time is wasted in researching responses, or worse, not following protocol

Figure out how to eliminate past impressions of UESC (example: 10-year ‘legal’ limit vs preferences on payback)

Utilize working groups to raise awareness of future intent that is not based on past projects
