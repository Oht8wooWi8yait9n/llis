NASA Lesson #0044: Electrical and Mechanical Equipment; Overhead Pipe Supports; Emergency Procedures for Critical Utility Systems; Utility Annex Emergency Power Shut Off Capability

Lesson ID: #0044 (Database ID: 44)
Date: 1991-12-05
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

A painter in the utility annex stepped on a pipe, causing it to fracture at the interface of an attached valve. The pipe remained attached to the valve, but the leak sprayed water onto the motor control center located beneath the pipe, causing a short circuit in the switch gear and a subsequent power outage for several facilities. This power outage resulted in the suspension of test operations that were being performed on the hubble space telescope.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Utility annex water lines routed above unprotected high voltage electrical equipment may rupture causing power outages and equipment failures.
  2. Pipes with less than adequate support will rupture or bend excessively when external forces are applied.
  3. Operations and maintenance instructions for critical utility systems must identify potential emergency conditions and provide emergency procedures.
  4. When there is no capability of shutting off utility annex power in an emergency, damaged equipment continues to operate without stability.

Recommendation(s)

  1. Provide splash guards or water resistant barriers for electrical and mechanical equipment subject to possible water damage.
  2. Place pipe supports within one foot of each change of direction and space the remaining supports to prevent excessive sag, bending and shear stress.
  3. Update OMI's for utility annexes and critical facilities, identifying emergency conditions and providing appropriate emergency procedures.
  4. Install local power disconnects at utility annexes for safing power during emergency situations.
