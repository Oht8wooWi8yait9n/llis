NASA Lesson #0943: Fire Hazards- Linseed Oil

Lesson ID: #0943 (Database ID: 943)
Date: 2001-05-07
Organization: Johnson Space Center
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

An employee placed wadded paper towels soaked with linseed oil inside their vehicle on the floorboard pending later disposal. The paper towels ignited and caused a fire in the vehicle. The fire was extinguished before any major damage. Further inspection of the area found wadded paper towels soaked with linseed oil that were disposed in a trash container. The trash container bag was retrieved and the paper towels were disposed properly before a fire could ignite. The paper towels retrieved were extremely warm to the touch, indicating spontaneous combustion had already begun.

Lesson(s) Learned

Spontaneous heating occurs when a self-ignition combustible reacts with sufficient oxygen from the air. This reaction is known as auto-oxidation, and results in the rapid evolution of heat and may eventually cause ignition. The MSDS for linseed oil and the label information have explicit warnings about the possibility of spontaneous combustion.

Recommendation(s)

  1. Before using any material, read carefully all cautions and directions on product label before use. Since empty containers typically retain residue, follow all label warnings after container is empty.
  2. Read your material's safety data sheet and become familiar with its hazardous properties and countermeasures.
  3. Oily rags or waste and other oily materials can cause spontaneous combustion fires if not handled properly; therefore, immediately after use, and before disposal or storage, you can,
  • Store rags in a metal container with a tight fitting cover until disposal. Keep container away from other flammable items, OR
  • Spread out all oily rags to dry by flattening them out to their full size in a well-ventilated place, OR
  • Hand wash them thoroughly with water and detergent and rinse (do not use washing machine which may trap vapors), OR
  • Immerse rags in water before disposal in a sealed waste container.
