Driving Event / Description

SLS Program Preliminary Design Review (PDR)

    The SLS Program conducted a PDR in June and July of 2013.  The review focus was on meeting the success criteria for the review per MPR 7123.1B.  Review item discrepancies (RIDs) for the SLS PDR were written against success criteria versus prior programs which focused on writing RIDs against specific documents under review.   The review approach included a combined technical and safety review, the use of MPR 7123.1B milestone success criteria, and eleven discipline review teams.   Success criterion 9 was tailored for safety and mission assurance to guide the combined technical and safety assessments.

    Data package documents were mapped to success criteria to support the review approach of writing RIDs against the PDR success criteria rather than against documentation. For example, multiple data items, which were mapped to a success criterion, could be used to support a success criteria RID. 

   The planning process of the PDR included development of a review plan that detailed the review process which included review team lead, review member, Tabletop/Pre-screening lead, LSE, Pre-Board, and Board responsibilities, the scope of the PDR, the review schedule, the documentation maturity matrices, document mapping to entrance criteria to support the PDR readiness assessment, document mapping to success criteria to support  development of pre-RIDs and comments, and the PDR data package listing that also included reference documentation. The implementation and execution process of the PDR review plan included review training, logistics for rooms, parking, review and Tabletop/Pre-screening team scheduling, web portal and review team dashboard development to support review communication,  review team lead and review team member selection, and conduction of the review. Review conduction included review of products and development of pre-declared RIDs,   discipline team and  Tabletop/Pre-screening reviews , screening, dispositioning of RIDS which included negotiating the terms of RID closure, the compilation of the review results, and the presentation of the review results to decisional authorities for approval.

Post PDR Review Success Evaluation

   Following the completion of the PDR, a PDR Pause and Learn (PaL) activity and review effectiveness survey were administered to the participants of the review.  The PaL assessment included a survey, focus groups, interviews, and a quantitative data assessment of the pre-RID flow through discipline team and Tabletop/Pre-screening .  Lessons learned gained from the PaL and survey are described below.

   The PaL and survey covered a quantitative assessment of the Pre-RID and RID process, the PDR training, and included a PaL interview and a PaL focus group exercise.  The objective of the PaL activity was to gain knowledge from the experience of conducting this review and to learn and apply the lessons gained to the next review cycles.  The PaL activity included all information gathering methods: survey, interview, and focus groups.  PaL activities focused on gathering constructive feedback about the reviews rather than developing specific solutions for the next review. 

    A Likert scale survey was conducted to determine if  the review was planned and executed well in regards to the review process, logistics, customer service, information management, and the MSFC RID System. The survey was designed to be conducive to constructive feedback and easy results compilation. The training provided leading up to the review was surveyed as well through MSFC ‘s training organization for additional constructive feedback.   

    Focus groups for Review Team Leads, planning, logistics, RID initiator, developers of corrective actions, and internal EV71 planning were conducted to gather constructive feedback not solutions. The focus groups were facilitated by persons without a direct link to the review. In addition, semi-structured interviews of several Review Team Leads were also conducted to gather more detailed qualitative data.  

    All focus group and interview notes were compiled and assessed for common themes.  The common repetitive themes were condensed into recommended results, goals, and areas to improve by review topic area (planning, review process, logistics, customer service, information management, and the MSFC RID System).  The condensed qualitative data was further condensed into a PaL top ten list. Quantitative observations were used in order to understand the acceptance rate of pre-RIDs between the discipline review, tabletop review( prescreening), and screening. All results, goals, and areas to improve were validated by Chief Engineer, Program, and S&MA peer review.

    The team evaluated the utility and flow of using the RID tool and they gained insight into the logistics, support, and scheduling of reviews that can aid future milestone review planners.   The PaL also looked at the SLS Review Portal page which was used as the main communication gateway for key review activities, contacts, review data package links, training packages and videos, as well as other key review links. 

 Summary of Post-PDR Execution Evaluation

    The PaL and open ended-question survey observances suggested that the portal page needed to be  decluttered in order to become more user friendly.  The Review Team Lead dashboard accessed via the SLS Review Portal was used for Review Director and Cross-Review Team communication.  The dashboard was observed to have been used by half of the review team leads.  The discussion board capability for the review was used infrequently.  Minor restructuring of the portal page and review team lead dashboard has the potential to improve reviewer usability.  More training on the importance of use of the dashboard and discussion board also has the potential to ultimately improve the review communication process.

    The specific survey topic areas were:

a.      The PDR Review Process

b.      Logistics

c.      Review Customer Service

d.      Information Management

e.      MSFC RID System (RID Tool)

f.       Review training (review process, RID Tool, and limited data rights) 

    Overall, the data showed that the PDR was well executed in all areas.  The recommendations for changes are relatively minor.  The training survey indicated that training was satisfactorily conducted, with a few refining recommendations provided.  In short, the PDR was considered successful.

End Result: A successful review. The overall Likert scale survey for planning and review execution results indicated the review was planned and conducted satisfactorily with a median of 3.7.   The mean and median minus the planning and logistics team inputs resulted in a rating of 3.9. Neutral and agree were 3 and 4 respectively on the Likert scale survey for planning and review execution. The overall training survey result was a median of 2 which corresponds to “agree” indicating training was conducted satisfactorily.

 

Lesson(s) Learned

1.      Milestone reviews are first and foremost about Data, Communication, Collaborative Team work, team leadership, and organization of work tasks and products.  Organizers of milestone review events must plan to enable a smooth flow of activity and data throughout the event.

Roles, Responsibilities, and Commitments

2.      In some review areas, planning team roles and responsibilities were not clearly defined.

3.      The external reviewer process and expectations were not clearly defined.

4.      In some cases, discipline points of contact and /or document developers were difficult to locate for support during the screening process due to events such as planned vacations.

Tabletops

5.      Success criteria tabletops or prescreening did not significantly reduce the number of pre-RIDs going to screening. 

6.      Pre-Declared RID actionees were not assigned prior to kickoff. Pre-Declared RID actionees were assigned toward the end of the screening process. This resulted in the developer/assignee of the corrective action having less time to develop a corrective action response.

7.      The SE Success Criteria Tabletop carried 51% of the pre-RID load. This was an indicator that for future reviews additional support would be required for the systems engineering discipline.

8.      The Tabletop/Pre-screening activity had an acceptance rate of Pre-RIDS of 90%.  This is a high percentage of Pre-RIDs that were sent forward to screening indicating that the Tabletop/Pre-screening process was not working as well as expected to reduce the number of Pre-RIDs going into screening.

Screening

9.      A screening schedule for RID Discussion at the beginning of the screening meetings would have been helpful and kept the first days of screening time focused.  

MSFC RID System and Data Access Issues

10.  The MSFC RID System would benefit from additional fields for comments, expected closure date, and, and a final corrective action.

11.  The data access process was a complicated lengthy process due to items such as NASA identity requirements in IDmax if you did not have a NASA ID, RID Tool IDmax requirement to access the MSFC RID System, and training requirements for the RID Tool and Limited Data Rights.

12.  The review portal page contained a lot of review information on one page.  Some reviewers had difficulty locating the desired data links due to the amount of information on the page.

Logistical Support Issues

  1. Customer service rooms were not fully utilized.  The use of fewer Customer Service rooms should be considered for CDR.
  2. Conducting table top reviews across disparate and physically dislocated personnel was a challenge.  The team used WebEx telecons, but not video teleconferencing. 

The prescreening review location(s) were distributed in two primary buildings in the same building complex.  As a result, it was difficult to easily walk to other prescreening/tabletop locations since they were distributed on different floors in two different buildings.  Some people needed to be in multiple prescreening locations at the same time.

Recommendation(s)

Summary

1.      Reevaluate the use of Success Criteria Tabletops/Prescreening.

2.      Clarify planning team roles and responsibilities to reduce any overlap or task confusion.

3.      Define process and expectations for external reviewers.

4.      Consider data access process improvement.

5.      Assign Pre-Declared RID actionees prior to Kickoff.

6.      Develop a backup discipline point of contacts and a backup document developer list.

7.      Assess if a central location for CDR is possible.

8.      Reformat portal page to be more user friendly.

9.      Add additional fields for comments, expected closure date, and a final corrective action in the MSFC RID System.

10.  Develop detailed RID discussion schedules to improve time management during screening and/or dispositioning meetings.

 

Detailed

  1. Logistics:

a.       Avoid holidays and school vacations (Fall and Spring Break).

b.      Limit vacation time during a milestone review.

c.       Have a goal to balance the success criteria review team workload.

d.      The pre-Board (final approval) should have a template and it should be available early in the review process to allow the secretariats to populate the pre-Board agenda as they go.  This would level the work load needed to prepare pre-Board charts, which eventually become Board charts.

e.       It is preferable to lock down documents at least 6 months prior to the review, or in accordance with the approved schedule.  Late documentation throws the planned work load off balance. 

f.        Work out all issues with the data access process prior to review kick off.

 

g.       Agendas, room lists, all logistical information that would help participants know what is going on over the entire review should be available online.  More than one avenue of communication of the logistics is preferable. 

 i.      Co-Locating the Review Participants is Highly Recommended.

  1. Planning Review Events:
  2. Planners should evaluate the use of Tabletops/Pre-screening versus other forums for reviewing and discussing technical data in a technical milestone review. 

i.      Planners should consider the need and benefit of Tabletop review of RIDs.  All three screening process steps may be able to be collapsed to one.

ii.      Concurrent Tabletops may overload some reviewers who cannot attend all relevant tabletops.

  1. The timing of the review should be in conjunction with the readiness for the review, and should consider the SRB schedule in mind, as well as the timing between element and vehicle reviews.
  2. Allow more time for development of corrective actions.

i.      Corrective action assignees were pressed to try to get corrective actions into the RID tool prior to discipline review team disposition.

  1. Dispositioning time should be at least one week.
  2. On Comments:

 i.      The RID Tool needs to have a comment graphical interface button added during each major RID Tool step to capture comments.  Currently comments can only be captured at screening using the “cancel as comment” feature.

 ii.      Comments should be established as formal comments prior to screening.

 

  1. Team Leader Roles
  2. Team Leader roles and responsibilities need to be clear and distinct. 
  3. A greater focus on Team leader roles and responsibilities should be provided in review training.
  4. Assign Actionees to pre-declared RIDs prior to kick off.
  5. Tabletop review agendas should include a schedule of the pre-RIDs to be discussed.

 i.      The agenda should be available to other Review Team Leads.

  1. Team Leads should know the review schedule and when products/results are due. 

 i.      Team Leads should evaluate and plan their team activities to coordinate with the overall schedule.

 ii.      Team Leads should communicate the planned schedule to all team members and the review director.

  1.  All Team Leads should provide criteria assessments.
  2. Team Leads need to be proactive.
  3. Transferring RIDs between teams was difficult and had to be done offline.  Team Leads did not always coordinate with each other.
  4. Pre-Board charts should be pre-reviewed by all the Team Leads if possible.
  1. Training
  2. Team may have needed more training on what criteria based reviews are versus the traditional document based reviews.
  3. Team leaders and deputy team leaders need to be trained in the scope and execution of their roles and responsibilities, including the hands-on use of the RID tool.
  4. Limited Rights Data training may need to be adapted to the roles of 1) reviewer and 2) document developer.
  1. Approach, Criteria
  2. For technical reviews, the leading authority needs to state whether or not programmatic material is under review. 
  3. If programmatic material or considerations are under review, the programmatic success criteria must be provided.
  4. If it is a technical review, focus on the technical criteria, and not on programmatic criteria.
  5. Clearly define the process and expectations for external reviewers.
  1. Communication
  2. IT Issues

 i.      Do not schedule server maintenance during a review.

  ii.      Data access (see Limited Rights data access comment above)

1.      Access had too many gates.

2.      There was a lack of access to some documents, such as Level III documents.

 iii.      On the RID tool:

1.      RID numbers should remain consistent and traceable throughout the process.

 iv.      Make portal page or central location for information user friendly.

  1. Review Team Members should work with the Developer if possible prior to submitting comments via a spreadsheet or putting comments into the RID tool. 
  2. DLEs can help keep the developers informed about changes and comments made to their documents.

If the RID changes after the developer has entered the corrective action into the RID tool, those responsible for the change need to coordinate with the developer to ensure proper handling and traceability of the CA.

Evidence / Additional Context

The lessons learned are being used to plan CDR for SLS
