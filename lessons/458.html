NASA Lesson #0458: Coordination of Facilities Operations Between Facility Users and Providers of Support (e.g., maintenance) Services

Lesson ID: #0458 (Database ID: 458)
Date: 1995-09-17
Organization: Johnson Space Center
Program Phase: None
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

During an engineering test, organizations external to the test organization were performing maintenance activities in the test vicinity. Welding was being performed in a test facility, which resulted in the deactivation of the facility's fire alarm bells during the first few days of a test. This condition was discovered by chance observation and not during inspection nor during normal pre-test activities.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Coordination of discrete operations in a facility is needed to preclude the deactivation of required emergency systems especially during a hazardous operation or test activity.
  2. Coordination must be an integrated process involving at least one person knowledgeable in the facility's planned day to day operations.

Recommendation(s)

  1. A single point of contact for each JSC facility was implemented to ensure that all operations in the facility (including access)are controlled and managed to ensure appropriate coordination of operations. This point of contact is described in the Facility Manager Functions covered by a human resources letter dated 11/30/94.
  2. Pre- and post-test fire technicians' checklists for Bldgs 32 and 33 have been generated.
  3. The use of emergency information signs must be in accordance with emergency action plans to preclude misleading information or direction being implemented.
  4. Fire alarm system disarming procedures now require an advance approval of a request from the Facility Manager and from work control prior to activation and deactivation of such systems. The procedures for these requests are JJMU-1001, Fire Suppression System Arming Checklist and JJMU-1004 Fire Suppression System Deactivation Checklist.
