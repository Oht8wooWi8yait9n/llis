NASA Lesson #34202: Launch Equipment Test Facility (LETF) Vehicle Motion Simulator (VMS) Hydraulic System Failure Resulted in $500K in Electrical System Damage.

Organization: Kennedy Space Center

Driving Event / Description

During hydraulic system pressurization for LETF operations, a NOSHOK pressure gauge ruptured while operating at normal pressures. This resulted in approximately 75 gallons of hydraulic fluid being sprayed throughout the pump house. The oil intrusion damaged various pieces of electrical equipment, costing approximately $500,000 in damages. A full mishap investigation was conducted, and the resulting report was documented in the NASA Mishap Information System (NMIS) under tracking number #23-100895.

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. NASA Advisory Notice NA-KSC-2024-002 was created and released to notify other NASA Centers of the NOSHOK gauge failure. The notice was released through NANADARTS.
  2. The colocation of high dollar value electrical equipment and the high-pressure hydraulic system pumps revealed that KSC did not have requirements to assess worst-case consequences from fluid leaks. 
  • KSC updated design requirements documentation to assess fluid systems designs for the potential to damage adjacent high value equipment (HVE) due to worst-case catastrophic fluid system ruptures or leaks.
  1. Employees could not see inside pump house during operation and therfore did not easily discover the leak to shut the system down. 
  • Cameras were installed inside of the pump house to allow facility employees real-time visual access to the conditions inside of the pump house.
  • Installation of a shunt trip was also performed to enable a quick shutdown of the system remotely if an off- nominal condition is detected.
  1. Other same model gauges on the system were inspected and found to be in a degareded condition. Gauges were found with corrosion to their inside housing, complete loss of gauge glycerin, a bent pointer, and missing or embedded plugs.
  • Updated pressure system preventative maintenance documented procedures and training to include looking for common signs of wear and damage on pressure gauges.

Recommendation(s)

1.  Review  NASA Advisory Notice NA-KSC-2024-002 and take action if needed. 

2. Apply lessons learned in this mishap event to like systems and facilities that may be present at your Center/site.
