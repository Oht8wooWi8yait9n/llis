NASA Lesson #4620: Standard Change Order Cost Proposals Format

Lesson ID: #4620 (Database ID: 4620)
Date: 2010-12-20
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Approval
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Acquisition / procurement strategy and planning, Business processes, Configuration and data management
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

A construction contractor on a NASA project is directed by the Contracting Officer to add scope (additional work) to the project; then the contractor submits their cost proposal for the added work to the Government. This contractor’s proposal is evaluated by the NASA construction management team and if there are differences of opinion, a negotiation is held to clarify scope and imbalances between the parties' estimated costs. In the past, NASA/KSC has not typically directed the contractor to use any one type of standardized format when submitting cost data for an authorized change. On smaller projects with few change orders, it has not been a problem to evaluate the change in whatever format the contractor wishes to use, as long as all the pertinent data has been included.

In the course of the NASA on-site management of the construction of the Ares I Mobile Launcher (ML) at Kennedy Space Center (KSC), the change orders were numerous and tended to be technically complicated, due to the magnitude of the ML project (~$140M). The project schedule was also very tight. Turnaround time to get the change orders negotiated and finalized was critical. Each of the subcontractors working under the prime used a different format. Some change order cost proposals being submitted contained seven or more independently formatted worksheets.

Initial change order proposal requests from the contractor were being presented in different formats depending on which subcontractor was preparing the cost information. This made it very difficult for the evaluation team to follow, and much time was spent understanding what information was or wasn’t included in the contractor's figures.

The NASA team dictated the use of a specific Excel format and requisite supporting data. This expedited review and facilitated negotiation. The prime contractor and subcontractors initially resisted but then fully endorsed the usage of standard formatting and suggested it be utilized on all construction change orders.

Lesson(s) Learned

A standard change order proposal format allowed improved understanding of the scope of the work by both parties and resulted in an expedited and more amicable resolution to the change order negotiation.

Recommendation(s)

Cost proposal format should be dictated as part of the contract.
