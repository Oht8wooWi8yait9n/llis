NASA Lesson #1417: Project Management: Effective Project Management Training

Lesson ID: #1417 (Database ID: 1417)
Date: 2004-01-01
Organization: Ames Research Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Aeronautics Research, Human Exploration and Operations, Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The PM T&D Coordinator created a T&D program that was well suited to and supported by the scientists and engineers of the Center. The Program was designed to work within the Center's culture and to provide management training valued by both scientists and engineers. To achieve this, the T&D Coordinator investigated knowledge gaps and existing interest in training within the community, collected feedback from participants in training activities and personally observed courses and participant response. Through these actions the PM T&D coordinator learned what types of PM training, content and learning environment would work for the ARC community.

Key Words/Phrases:

Professional development, training, culture, distant learning, environmental amenties

Lesson(s) Learned

In order to create an effective PM T&D Program attention must be given to both the training content and learning environment. The training content that worked best for the Center's PM community had several characteristics including: local relevance and application, narrow focus, minimal history and theory, and in-class “hands-on” work supported by experienced and credible instructors. The key was to provide training that the students felt confident in and could apply immediately to their work. The factors that contributed the most to the student's training environment addressed issues such as the student's schedule, travel requirements, in-class comfort, attention span, and visual and audio stimulation. The key was to provide a training experience that fostered student participation.

Recommendation(s)

When planning PM T&D activities for the center, consideration should be given to both the training content and the training environment, specifically:

  1. Training content should address an immediate need such as how to prepare a proposal or create an effective WBS (Work Breakdown Structure) and should:
  1. Include case studies, simulations or “hands-on” work.
  2. Have a focused aim geared at specific problem solving techniques.
  3. Avoid extensive background or history material.
  4. Be supported by an instructor's or guest speaker's (preferably a recognized expert including those outside the Agency) personal experience
  1. Training delivery, environment, and experience should be geared toward the community's schedule, comfort, and culture. In particular:
  1. Courses should not be scheduled for more than four days; two days are preferable.
  2. Courses should have minimal pre-work.
  3. Classes should be kept small, preferably thirty enrollees or less. The size should facilitate in class teamwork and individual participation.
  4. Lengthy lectures should be avoided. All lectures should include visual aids and frequent breaks to help participants maintain their attentiveness
  5. Comfortable and convenient locations, seating arrangements that facilitate teamwork and dialogue, and amenities such as refreshments should be used to enhance the training's effectiveness.
  6. Distance learning (online training) should be minimized and used only for very short awareness and refresher types of material. Effective development of PM skills is better supported through a different learning environment that includes interaction with a speaker, instructor or facilitator and other training participants.
