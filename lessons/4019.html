NASA Lesson #4019: Proprietary Elevator Procurement

Lesson ID: #4019 (Database ID: 4019)
Date: 2010-09-29
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Implementation
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Ground support systems, Logistics and maintenance, Requirements critical to costing and cost credibility, Acquisition / procurement strategy and planning, Engineering design and project processes and standards, Long term sustainability and maintenance planning, Systems analysis - cost analysis
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

All five elevators in the OSB I at the KSC are proprietary, even though proprietary was not required. All other elevators at the Launch Complex 39 area had been upgraded and were nonproprietary. A minor modification to the OSB I elevators (such as installing five limit switches and reprogramming five proprietary elevators) was quoted at $35,000.00. The same work was quoted at approximately $2500.00 for nonproprietary elevators.

Lesson(s) Learned

Parts and maintenance costs on proprietary elevators are substantially higher than for nonproprietary systems. On occasion, procurements fail to specify nonproprietary elevators. In those instances, contractors sometimes bid proprietary installations. Because proprietary elevators are more expensive to install and maintain and because proprietary designs are not required at KSC, all future elevator installations should be nonproprietary.

Recommendation(s)

  • Do not procure proprietary elevator systems.
  • Review requirements and consider cheaper options when making a purchase.
