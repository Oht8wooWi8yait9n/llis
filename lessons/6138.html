NASA Lesson #6138: Advantages of a consistent, multi-element nomenclature for all data measurements

Driving Event / Description

 In the Space Shuttle Program, all data measurements that were taken during launch vehicle operation, called MSIDs, were captured with consistent, structured data naming conventions.  For example, with the Space Shuttle Main Engine (SSME), having consistent MSIDs facilitated flight data processing since programmers and engineers did not have to retain a knowledge of various naming conventions. This is in contrast to other programs such as the Delta program which used multiple naming conventions.

Lesson(s) Learned

During the entire shuttle program, consistent idnetification numbers (MSID's) were used for instrumentation recorded on the Shuttle. These names included enough information to determine the shuttle subsystem (engine, vehicle, tank, ground, etc.), the data stream (and sample rate), and the type of measurement (temperature, pressure, etc.). This allowed consistent real time and post-flight analysis from event to event.  Analysis of trends and data reduction of across 30 years of Shuttle Program data was greatly faciltated by the consistency of the measurement identification numbers naming convention.

Recommendation(s)

When determining the instrumentation required by each subsystem, make sure to have one authorized body to maintain the common naming conventions.
