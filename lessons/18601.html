Driving Event / Description

Vibration overtest close call of the Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) mission Power System Electronics Box

Lesson(s) Learned

  1. Hard shutdowns are common protection mechanisms for vibration hardware and they can impose an undesirable shock to the test article. 
  2. Random signal inputs into vibration tables may be perceived as excessive accelerations in certain conditions. 
  3. In many cases, a vibration overtest event may happen prior to the planned start of the test, causing an overtest stress where the vibration levels are not measured. 
  4. Small signs of problems and out of family behavior of vibration equipment frequently indicates that a serious problem is emerging. 
  5. An environmental test performed without expectation or preparation for a failure or anomaly is likely to be a test performed in vain. 

Recommendation(s)

  1. Go through all efforts to prevent hard shutdowns whenever possible.  Be sure that the equipment prioritizes safety of the test article over protection of the test equipment when testing valuable flight hardware. 
  2. When performing random vibration test, ensure that the equipment recognizes that input signals are random and does not shut the test down based on input discontinuities associated with a random signal. 
  3. Always ensure that real-time acceleration measurements are collected from the moment the system is powered up with a test article in place. 
  4. Take note and follow up on all glitches and out of family behavior of vibration equipment. Do not use equipment that has unresolved problems. 
  5. Go into every test expecting a failure or anomaly and be prepared to analyze and learn from what happened.
