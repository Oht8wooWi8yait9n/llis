NASA Lesson #7136: International Space Station (ISS) Hardware Disposition Project

Lesson ID: #7136 (Database ID: 7136)
Date: 2012-12-01
Organization: Kennedy Space Center
Program Phase: Implementation &raquo; Phase F
Mission Directorate: Human Exploration and Operations
Topic / Category: Disposal, Configuration control and data management, Mission concepts and life-cycle planning, Business processes, Configuration and data management, Program planning / development and management

Driving Event / Description

To effectively manage and implement Post-Shuttle disposition activities for identified ISS hardware, an Integrated Disposition Team (IDT) was formed. The team consisted of the Checkout, Assembly and Payload Processing Services (CAPPS) contractors and its subcontractors along with NASA-KSC personnel from various disciplines. Dispositioned systems included downgraded flight hardware, major Ground Support Equipment (GSE) and processing equipment. In addition, Special Test Equipment (STE), specific facility subsystems (cranes and doors), and work platforms/stands were also dispositioned. A total of 28,193 line items were either excessed, retained, returned to support other programs, or transferred to the government. A small percentage of documentation close-out was also involved. The total estimated market value of the items dispositioned was 282 million dollars. Another 3,598 of user-controlled items in various locales of the Space Station Processing Facility (SSPF) high and low bays were also dispositioned.

Lesson(s) Learned

  • It was important to pursue and implement Lean/Accelerated Improvement workshops to develop tools and processes that create a high performance team. Each team member had to become familiar with each discipline’s processes; this allowed for increased productivity for the entire team as a whole.
  • Continual early coordination with the appropriate Program and Configuration management personnel was paramount to ensure timely release of Change Revision (CR) documentation. This was required for the downgrade of the flight hardware before it could actually undergo disposition.
  • Realize flexibility and change is inevitable! The Team remained flexible throughout the life of the project as additional PMNs/Systems were added for disposition. Realize process sequences may change. For example, the IDT discovered screening should be done earlier in the process to avoid possible re-work.

Recommendation(s)

  1. Pursue the early identification of items required to be “safed” (environmental hazards) and ensure they are properly identified on the schedule.
  2. The disposition team leader should form a Work in Progress (WIP) Board to allow for efficient data tracking, successful management and oversight of the project during actual day-to-day operations.
  3. Develop a strong relationship with your partners along with early inclusion of stakeholders to ensure timely disposition processing including the reduction of screening times where appropriate.
