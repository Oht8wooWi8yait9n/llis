NASA Lesson #33101: Displacement of E-1 High Pressure Liquid Hydrogen Vent Line to Flare Stack

Lesson ID: #33101 (Database ID: 33101)
Date: 2023-09-21
Organization: Stennis Space Center
Program Phase: Implementation
Topic / Category: Engineering Design, Mission Operations and Ground Support Systems, Cryogenic Systems, Energetic Materials - Explosive / Propellant / Pyrotechnic, Facilities, Ground Operations, Pressure Vessels, Test Facility, Advanced planning of safety systems, Configuration Change Control
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

The damage to the vent line is believed to have occurred during Thrust Chamber Assembly (TCA) testing on Thursday, September 21, during the first test of the day. The fuel systems being used for TCA testing are connected to the Low Pressure (LP) and High Pressure (HP) vent systems and are being used in Liquid Natural Gas (LNG) service. The Cell 3 interface bleed valve that discharges to the HP LH Vent system was inadvertently left at 100% open during the chill-in process for longer than the typical amount of time. Since this valve is usually at a lower set point or intermittently closed during the chill-in process, more than the typical amount of LNG flowed into the vent line as pre-test procedures continued as normal.

 

Lesson(s) Learned

The root causes of this Undesired Outcome (UO) are identified as follows: the project design processes did not identify the vent system for additional evaluation, the Process Hazard Analysis (PHA) did not identify the hazard, and there was complacency related to the vent line. 

The design process looked at the adequacy of the Run Tank and connected run line piping, and the HP Flare Stack for LNG service, but it did not evaluate the vent piping between the run system and the Flare Stack.  During the safety review of the design, which has multiple aspects, the hazard related to liquid and the increased loads was not identified.  Finally, the complacency related to the vent line developed over time.  The vent line was not maintained or inspected between tests or on a regular basis.  Also, the system identity includes LH, so liquid in the system was not seen as an issue.  Anecdotally, LH has been seen discharging from the flare stack, so system integrity was assumed to be adequate for current test operations.

Contributing factors for the failure are related to the inadequacy of the HP LH Vent System for LNG. Originally, the vent system was design for hydrogen service, where the worst-case loads were from venting of the HP LH Run tank at maximum pressure. LNG has a density much greater than gaseous hydrogen, so produces much higher momentum loads on the support structure when moved at similar velocities. More capable support structure would have helped but likely not prevented the UO. Lastly, had the time between the vent line being overflown with LNG and test been longer, the vent line would have warmed up enough to vaporize all the liquid in the northern section that was damaged.

Additionally, the HP vent line was overflown with LNG during chill-in because the operator left the bleed valve 100% open for longer than intended. This action was not constrained, and at the time, not seen as an issue. If liquid had been identified as an issue, the Detailed Operating Procedures (DOP) would have likely identified constraints for chill-in procedure. Moreover, if the Cell 3 bleed had not been connected to the HP LH Vent System, this UO would have been prevented. Using the HP Vent system for disposal of ambient cryogenic propellants, even liquid hydrogen, is ill-advised.

Recommendation(s)

Disciplined configuration control practices are vital to ensure that facility systems are built and maintained to the specified design. It is important to consider all the differences between the commodity used and how it will affect the entire system. Once these effects are known, they need to be clearly understood by operators and documented. Operators and technicians need to understand the limits of the system for when they need to adjust procedure for mission success. This can be difficult when encountering a failure of imagination. Additionally, when changing configuration, all individual systems must be analyzed even when a system is known to be robust.
