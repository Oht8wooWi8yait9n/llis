Driving Event / Description

The Space Shuttle Program Transition and Retirement team had to manage the disposition of property that did not obviously have the potential for recycling, including x-ray film, paper, items containing lead, and property with the potential to contain small amounts of PCBs.

Lesson(s) Learned

Property planned for disposal at NASA Centers and contractors can often be recycled or sold for scrap, but some property that can be recycled is not always obvious and Programs may have limited awareness about recycling. This includes scrap metal and electronics, which are ordinarily recycled, as well as paper, x-ray film and even materials containing lead or PCBs if properly handled. Identifying potential recyclers requires some additional effort but can recoup funds or save disposal costs.

Recommendation(s)

Programs with property to dispose of should consider recycling when possible, and can contact environmental and property offices at the appropriate Center or the NASA Principal Center for Recycling and Sustainable Acquisition. Electronics can be recycled through UNICOR (Federal Prison Industries is commonly referred to by its trade name UNICOR), and there are companies that can accept material that contain lead, chromium, and/or PCBs. Recyclers are also available that can accept x-ray film and paper and provide appropriate controls to ensure that the material is destroyed. Recycling will not always be the most efficient method of disposition, but additional effort to identify potential recyclers can result in cost savings from recouping scrap value and/or savings in disposal costs and landfill space as well as support NASA’s sustainability efforts.
