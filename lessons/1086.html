NASA Lesson #1086: Lack of Close Call Reporting and Corrective Action Development at GSFC

Lesson ID: #1086 (Database ID: 1086)
Date: 2000-03-24
Organization: Goddard Space Flight Center
Program Phase: None
Mission Directorate: Science
Source: View Official NASA LLIS Entry (live system)

Driving Event / Description

A NASA Mishap Investigation Board (MIB) was tasked with investigating the perceived lack of corrective actions and lack of close call reporting of two incidents into the NASA Mishap Reporting System: the GSFC Building 24 pressurized steam line close call of August 19, 1999, and the GSFC Building 3/13/14 electrical manhole close call of December 26, 1998. For more detailed information about these two close calls, please refer to separately published lessons learned findings for each close call incident in the NASA Lessons Learned Information System (LLIS).

Lesson(s) Learned

The primary cause of the lack of close call reporting of these incidents was a lack of awareness of what constitutes a close call and the fact that close calls should be reported into the NASA Mishap Reporting System via the 1627 Form. Personnel involved in the pressurized steam line incident were unaware that the 1627 Form should have been submitted. The delayed submittal of this form was due to the pending appointment of the MIB. Personnel involved in the electrical manhole incident did not believe it was a close call. The MIB identified two significant contributing causes to these perceptions. The Code 220, Code 227 and NASA level guidance documents on close call/mishap reporting as well as the mishap Form 1627 itself focus on mishaps. Most of the guidance provided is mishap-specific such as attending to the injured and securing the scene, things that do not typically exist in close calls. While Code 220 and 227 guidance do require close call reporting, testimony indicated that it had become common practice to address close calls as an internal issue (in the branch/section in which they occurred) and not pass the information up the management chain or to the GSFC Safety and Environmental Branch.

Corrective actions were developed for both of these close call incidents. However, in the pressurized steam line incident the corrective actions were not adequate to prevent recurrence since they did not address the root causes of the incident.

Employees cannot follow policies they are not aware of or are not clear. Even though NASA policies do require the reporting of close calls into the NASA mishap reporting system, if that information is not passed on to all employees in an easily understood and visible manner it will not be followed.

Resolution of close calls is best accomplished through close cooperation between the organization in which the close call occurred and the Center's safety organization. Each organization brings unique knowledge skills and abilities to the table that can be used to determine what went wrong and what can be done to prevent recurrence.

If close calls are not investigated and addressed in a timely manner, the possible benefits derived from them (i.e., preventing mishaps caused by similar events) are lost.

Lack of Knowledge of What Constitutes a Close Call: In both the Pressurized Steam Line and Electrical Manhole close calls, a general lack of knowledge of the definition of what constitutes a close call was evident. Witnesses testified in both cases that they did not consider the incidents close calls. In the Steam line case, the Mechanical Maintenance Shop (MMS) worker did consider it a close call and did report it to his supervisor (as required) and the Safety and Environmental Branch (Code 205.2). The MMS supervisor informed the General Foreman and he brought the MMS and CPP groups together to solve the issue. They were not aware that a close call should be reported on a NASA Mishap Reporting Form (1627 Form). The MMS workers expected Code 205.2 to investigate and assist in resolving the issue. Code 205.2 expected Code 220 to file the 1627 Form and take action to resolve the issue. When the 1627 Form was filled out 2 months later it appears to have been done without the information originally provided to Code 205.2 by the MMS worker. In the Electrical manhole incident, the supervisor did not consider the incident a close call and therefore did not consider taking any official action.

Discussion of Close Call Reporting In Guidance Documents: The MIB reviewed the following documents to determine if they provided adequate instructions on how to handle close calls: A) Code 227 Safety Manual, Reference E; B) GSFC Facilities Management Division (Code 220) Safety and Health Program Plan (S&HPP), Reference F; C) NPD 862 1.1.G, NASA Mishap Reporting and Investigating Policy, Reference A; and D) NHB; 1700.1 (-VI-B), NASA Safety Policy and Requirements Document, Reference B.

In general, the documents coverage of close calls is sparse. Close calls are identified as being one of the types of incidents covered, however, the majority of the guidance focuses heavily on aspects of mishaps that are not part of close calls (i.e., injuries, emergency situations). Discussion of close call reporting in the Code 227 Safety Manual is particularly unclear. A close call or near miss is not separately defined but is included as part of an incident definition. The steps to be taken by personnel involved in incidents and their supervisors focus on mishaps. This could lead readers to believe that close calls are not included in reporting policies. The Code 220 S&HPP's discussion of close calls is nearly identical to that included in NPD 8621.1G and NHB 1700.1 (VI-B). All of these documents specify that close calls are to be included in reporting policies and on the 1627 Form.

Mishap Reporting Form: The NASA Mishap Report form (1627 Form) is also geared primarily towards mishaps. The only indication that the form is also used for reporting close calls is one box to be checked. The rest of the form is focused on reporting injuries and equipment damage, items not normally a part of close calls. The form design could mislead employees into believing that it is not for close calls, only mishaps.

GSFC Close Call Hazard Reporting System: During the course of interviews the Board asked witnesses about their knowledge of the new GSFC Close Call Hazard Reporting System. Most witnesses were aware of the new system and felt it to be easier to use than the 1627 form for close call reporting. The MIB concurs with this assessment.

Fear of Reprisals: Several Witnesses expressed an unwillingness to report close calls due to the fear of reprisals. Contractors were concerned with losing future contracts if they reported civil servants caused close calls. Even if contractors did it anonymously, they feared that it could still be ascertained who submitted the report. Note these 2 incidents the MIB investigated were submitted anonymously through the NSRS system but several witnesses were convinced they knew who the submitter was, and their guesses were different. Therefore, even anonymous submittals may have a negative impact on the submitter (or on those thought to be the submitter).

Lack of Corrective Actions Development: The MIB found that corrective actions were developed in both incidents commensurate: with the supervisor's perception of the incident and its level of severity. In the pressurized steam line incident, the corrective actions listed on the mishap report form were not adequate to address the incident. The actions called for the development of a Standard Operating Procedure for face-to-face meetings between Mechanical Maintenance Shop (MMS) and Central Power Plant (CPP) personnel when outages involve both shops, the incorporation of that procedure into the branch safety manual and refresher training for all personnel in lock out/tag out procedures. Face-to-face meetings did occur the day of the incident and did not prevent it. While lock out/tag out was not done correctly during the incident, that fact did not contribute to the failure to inform the contractor that the lines were not secured or the failure to secure the lines in a timely manner. In addition, the MIB could find no evidence that the corrective actions had been completed. In the electrical manhole incident the corrective actions were appropriate.

Recommendation(s)

MIB recommendations related to this issue include but are not limited to: Establish GSFC-level policy/procedure to implement NPD 8621.1G. Provide specific guidance addressing Code 205's responsibilities for investigation & documentation of mishap/close call investigations & conclusions. Develop a GSFC Close Call Hazard Reporting System Implementation Policy that includes: a detailed close call definition/explanation; an explanation of the types of incidents that should be reported, the type of corrective actions should be developed by the reporting organization, calls for notification of the nature of close calls and corrective actions to the reporting organization's management chain, and feedback on the results of Code 205's close call hazard reporting system to GSFC employees. Update the Code 227 Safety Manual to define close calls and near-misses separately from incidents and to list the steps to be taken in their reporting. Increase management involvement in Code 227 proactive investigation of close calls and development of appropriate corrective actions.
